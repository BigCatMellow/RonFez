# Fez Whatley as **The Nature Boy**

If one performance best demonstrates how badly the late-era stereotype can distort Fez Whatley's career, it may be **The Nature Boy**.

Fez's Ric Flair-inspired wrestling promo was not a minor impression that happened once in a character contest. It was a recurring performance language that appears across multiple eras of his radio life—from memories of the *Ron & Ron* years, through WNEW, into the launch of the XM version of *Ron & Fez*, and still years later during drinking shows and the Dave/Fez character competitions.

The basic performance borrowed the cadence, self-mythology, swagger and explosive `WOOOO!` of professional wrestler **Ric Flair, “The Nature Boy.”** But Fez did not simply recite a wrestling promo. He adapted the form to whatever the show needed: boasting about Ron & Fez, insulting people in the room, turning current events into wrestling rhetoric, or suddenly becoming a version of himself who could talk with total authority and momentum.

That last point is historically important.

> **Nature Boy Fez is evidence of Fez as a commanding performer.**

Any history that jumps directly from “Fez was Ron's sidekick” to late-era silence without giving Nature Boy serious space loses a major part of who Fez was on the radio.

---

## What the impression sounded like

The recurring structure was recognizably Flair-like:

- prolonged, theatrical delivery;
- escalating boasts;
- rhythmic repetition;
- wrestling-promoter language;
- exaggerated wealth/status imagery;
- contempt for opponents;
- sudden shouted punctuation;
- the famous Flair-style **“Woooo!”**;
- a recurring idea that listeners did not have to like Ron & Fez but would have to recognize/love what they were doing.

The exact wording changed. That flexibility is part of what made it more than an impression.

Fez could use the **Nature Boy voice as a comic engine**.

# I. Florida / *Ron & Ron* roots

The exact first Nature Boy performance has **not yet been recovered**, so the archive should not invent a debut date.

But the character clearly reaches back before WNEW.

A 2015 *Ron & Fez* retrospective week reached all the way back into Tampa/*Ron & Ron* material. The Friday program explicitly included a segment identified as:

> **Fez is The Nature Boy: Marge Schott**

Source:
- https://theinterrobang.com/ron-and-fez-will-host-a-look-back-at-their-career-all-week/

This is one of the strongest surviving anchors for Nature Boy's **Florida-era ancestry**.

Later listeners who heard *Ron & Ron* in Florida independently remember Fez doing the Ric Flair/Nature Boy performance in the 1990s, often with intense or anthemic music underneath. Those memories are valuable, but they remain later listener testimony until more original *Ron & Ron* audio is recovered.

### Current safe conclusion

> **Nature Boy predates the New York Ron & Fez era and was already part of Fez's performance repertoire in the Florida/Ron & Ron world.**

Exact first date: **unresolved**.

# II. WNEW: Nature Boy becomes part of Fez's New York identity

By the WNEW years, Nature Boy was not obscure trivia.

A March 9, 2007 contemporary fan discussion listing the things that defined *Ron & Fez* specifically includes both:

- **“The Ric Flair intro/Satellite of Love exit”**;
- **Fez's impression of “THE NATURE BOY!”**

Source:
- https://forums.thesmartmarks.com/topic/85421-ron-and-fez/

The distinction is useful. The listener remembers both the **show-production use** of Ric Flair/Nature Boy audio and **Fez performing the character himself**.

Memorial recollections after Fez's death similarly remember evenings on WNEW built around Fez doing Nature Boy alongside other classic active Fez material such as André the Giant.

Source:
- https://theinterrobang.com/listeners-and-fans-say-goodbye-to-fez-whatley/

### Historical meaning

Nature Boy belongs with **Fezetorials, Jan Brady, André the Giant and Fez's other early performance material** as evidence that Fez could seize the microphone rather than merely react to Ron.

# III. September 12, 2005 — the XM return

*Ron & Fez* debuted on XM 202 on **September 12, 2005**.

A full surviving copy of the first XM broadcast is publicly archived under that date.

Source:
- https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=er5ghoJjHNA

Later listeners specifically identify **Nature Boy as part of the XM-return intro**, remembering it as one of the first sounds of Ron and Fez coming back to New York/national satellite radio.

Source:
- https://www.reddit.com/r/ronandfez/comments/11uod7q

### Evidence boundary

The date of the first XM broadcast is strong.

The exact placement and construction of the Nature Boy intro should still be verified directly from the full tape before this project publishes a precise timestamp or claims a particular edit sequence as A-level fact.

Even at the current evidence level, the choice is historically apt:

> an old Fez performance identity was used to announce a new era of the show.

# IV. Drunk Nature Boy / “Fezbird”

One of the reasons Nature Boy remained useful is that it could appear **spontaneously**, especially when Fez was unusually uninhibited.

Listeners preserve multiple memories/uploads of **Drunk Nature Boy** performances.

A particularly famous XM example is remembered as Nature Boy delivered over **Lynyrd's “Free Bird”**, often called **“Fezbird.”** In a 2023 search for the episode, listeners eventually identified the underlying show as:

> **November 10, 2006**

The remembered setup is the anniversary period after Fez's heart attack, with Fez drinking and turning the people/gifts around him into material for a long improvised Nature Boy promo.

Source:
- https://www.reddit.com/r/ronandfez/comments/10vsz38

### Current evidence grade

- date identification: **C/E — later listener recovery**;
- existence of the performance: strongly remembered and circulated;
- precise segment chronology/timestamp: still needs direct full-show indexing.

This is an especially useful example because it shows that Nature Boy was not merely a scripted callback from the Florida years. Fez could still **generate fresh material inside the persona**.

# V. 2009 — Dave vs. Fez Character Showdown

By late 2009, Nature Boy had enough historical weight to be brought back as one of Fez's legacy weapons in the formal **Dave vs. Fez Character Showdown**.

The surviving RonFez.net File Library directly preserves:

- **December 9, 2009 — Fez vs. Dave Character Showdown**;
- **December 17, 2009 — Part Deux**;
- **December 22, 2009 — Character Showdown Finale**.

The archive description for the finale says Dave and Fez were allowed to bring out characters from the past and explicitly lists:

- Jan Brady;
- Sid Rabinowitz;
- Mike Francesa;
- André the Giant;
- **The Nature Boy**.

Source:
- https://www.ronfez.net/forums/downloads.php?do=cat&id=61

This is direct surviving RFNet archive evidence.

### Why this matters beyond the contest

The Dave/Fez rivalry increasingly raised an uncomfortable real-world question:

> Was Dave becoming the more active performer beside Ron?

The Character Showdown converted that tension into a formal game.

Nature Boy's presence in the finale matters because the contest was not simply asking Fez to invent something new.

It allowed Fez to reach backward into **the performer he had already proven he could be**.

# VII. Nature Boy as an antidote to “Silent Fez” memory

The most important reason to preserve Nature Boy prominently is not wrestling nostalgia.

It changes the biography.

Late R&F contains years in which Fez:

- struggled to enter conversations;
- experienced stage fright and anxiety;
- locked up on-air;
- sometimes went through extremely long quiet stretches;
- became the subject of Ron's attempts to understand why he could not perform normally.

A listener entering through those years can accidentally construct a false earlier Fez:

> Ron was always the talent and Fez was always the strange quiet sidekick.

Nature Boy makes that interpretation impossible.

At his best, Fez could:

- take command of the room;
- sustain a long comic voice;
- improvise insults and boasts;
- work rhythmically with music;
- manipulate volume and pacing;
- stay inside a character while responding to live events;
- generate enough momentum that Ron could simply enjoy what Fez was doing.

That contrast is essential to understanding why Fez's later performance difficulties were so striking to longtime listeners and to Ron himself.

# VIII. Relationship to Ric Flair and R&F wrestling culture

Nature Boy belongs inside a much larger wrestling influence on the show.

R&F repeatedly borrowed wrestling's language of:

- promos;
- heels and faces;
- factions;
- masks;
- championships;
- worked exits;
- “work” and “shoot” ambiguity;
- loser-leaves-town structures;
- live fight cards.

The Big ASS Night of Fights events, Al Dukes' exit framing, East Side Dave/Midnight Rider material, Big ASS Bash and the later project's own work/shoot vocabulary all exist in this larger tradition.

Nature Boy is the purest example of **Fez himself stepping directly into wrestling promo grammar**.

He was not merely discussing professional wrestling.

He was using its performance language as radio comedy.

# Core interpretation

Nature Boy should be understood as one of **Fez Whatley's signature performance modes**.

It connects the Florida performer, the WNEW co-host, the wrestling-obsessed R&F universe, the return on XM, drunk-show spontaneity, and the Dave-era anxiety over whether Fez could still access the performer he had once been.

That is why the impression matters historically even to somebody who does not care about wrestling.

**Nature Boy is one of the clearest surviving demonstrations of what Fez sounded like when he was completely, aggressively, joyfully in gear.**

Whoooo.
