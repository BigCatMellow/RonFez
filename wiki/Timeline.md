# Ron & Fez — Living Timeline

This is the reader-facing chronology. Dates are included when the repository has a defensible anchor; uncertain events remain labeled as such rather than being forced into a clean sequence.

For the full forensic chronology, see [`chronology/`](https://github.com/BigCatMellow/RonFez/tree/main/chronology).

---

# Before *Ron & Fez*

## 1987 — *Ron & Ron* begins in Tampa
Ron Bennington and Ron Diaz begin their major Florida radio partnership. The period develops several pieces of later R&F DNA: ensemble comedy, recurring personalities, large public events, and willingness to make the people around the program part of the program.

## Around 1990 — Todd Hillier enters the *Ron & Ron* world
Todd becomes the performer behind **Fez Whatley**, originally a flamboyant comic persona kept conceptually separate from Todd's publicly presented straight identity.

## 1997 — *Ron & Ron* ends
The partnership with Ron Diaz ends. Ron Bennington's eventual next full radio partner is not another conventional star but Fez, a figure who came from the surrounding ensemble.

## August 24, 1998 — Ron and Fez begin as a team in Daytona
The Ron/Fez partnership begins at WKRO in Daytona Beach.

# WNEW / New York — 2000–2003

## February 21, 2000 — *Ron and Fez Dot Com* debuts on WNEW
The New York era begins. The original internet-browsing premise matters less over time than the actual internet community that forms around the show.

## 2000–2001 — staff become characters
Billy Staples, Al Dukes, Black Earl and other production figures demonstrate the show's central personnel mechanism: the formal job can become less important than the person's behavior under Ron's attention.

## Approximately February 5, 2001 — RonFez.net institutional launch
RFNet's own tenth-anniversary post in 2011 points back to February 5, 2001. Some founding-era recollections loosely reference 2000/01, so precursor activity remains possible.

## Spring–fall 2001 — early RFNet culture develops rapidly
Surviving posts already show recurring handles, adjacent fan sites, board identity, ordinary-life conversation and early fan-created media.

## September 11, 2001 — full evening broadcast
Ron and Fez broadcast in New York after the attacks. RonFez.net later preserves the complete show as an explicit exception to its usual clip-oriented archive practice.

## 2001–2002 — New York Forever / physical community grows
Post-9/11 gatherings and bar crawls help turn online identities into physical relationships.

## February 22, 2002 — first Big ASS Night of Fights
A surviving ticket fixes the event at Downtown Bar & Club, 190 Main Street, Farmingdale, New York. The Al Dukes exit storyline demonstrates real employment change wrapped in wrestling-style narrative.

## May 17, 2002 — Big ASS Night of Fights II
A surviving multi-hour recording documents a remarkably dense early cast. GVac and Matty Fridays fight for the **RonFez.net Championship**, demonstrating how quickly board identities had become live-event characters.

## June 1, 2002 — celebrity-softball lead
A later archive discussion dates an early WNEW-era celebrity softball game to June 1 and remembers Tracy Morgan. Primary tape verification remains needed.

## July 15–16, 2002 — R&F call former producer Tuddle in the coffin/concrete stunt
A direct RFNet archive item preserves the crossover with Tuddle's later Orlando radio life.

## Christmas season 2002 — first RFNet Christmas party
The restored gallery preserves a **Christmas Party 2002** category. A later apparent participant independently identifies a labeled image as the first RF.NET X-Mas party and dates it to 2002. Dave & Buster's at the Palisades mall is a strong retrospective venue lead, not yet a contemporary-confirmed fact.

## 2002 — early cross-board culture already visible
Contemporary outside-board discussion stereotypes RFNet as unusually friendly/“candy coated,” references warning emails and staff-room moderation, and demonstrates that users already carried board citizenship between communities years before Board Gossip.

## October 11, 2002 — direct FoundryMusicJeff governance evidence
Jeff Shain / FoundryMusicJeff describes how he runs RonFez.Net, calls it his board, and refers to relevant board software as his own. This is the strongest current direct-period evidence of his early operational/technical authority.

## 2002–early 2003 — WNEW talk format destabilizes
The larger O&A/WNEW crisis contributes to the end of the talk experiment. The important R&F outcome is that the fan community, archive and running continuity already exist independently of one station.

# WJFK / Fairfax — 2003–2005

## June 2003 — Ron & Fez move to WJFK/Fairfax
The show becomes geographically smaller but more socially permeable.

## 2003 — Fairfax listener-to-character pipeline develops
Local callers, studio visitors, event participants and creators begin crossing categories. Figures associated with the period include Perrynoid, Cigar Sid/Sidcada, Crazy Jen, Don the Hypnotist, Mikeyboy and others.

## 2003 — WJFK fan-streaming ecosystem
Later listeners remember Big ASS Antenna, Live365, Radio BBQ, Winamp-style streams, emailed MP3s and homemade recording setups. The audience becomes part of the distribution infrastructure.

## September 18, 2003 — Hurricane Isabel
A U.S. congressional record later describes Ron and Fez devoting the program to storm coverage and acting as a conduit for listener reports about local conditions.

## 2003–2004 — dense WJFK event period
Events include the first pool tournament, Caddyshack Movie Night, poker, Hard Rock New York reunion, St. Pat's Spat, second pool tournament and local-band activity. Several currently rely on fan-compiled event history and still need tape-by-tape upgrades.

## 2004–2005 — Fez charity 5K lead
Historical R&F material says Fez ran annual charity 5Ks with fans. Exact race/charity identification remains open.

## 2005 — Friday Night Lights
Fez regularly gathers with listeners and friends at a Fairfax bar called **Thursdays**. The gatherings function more like an off-air clubhouse than a station remote.

## July 29, 2005 — final WJFK show and final Friday Night Lights
The local Fairfax period ends. Ron is remembered as attending the final Friday Night Lights.

## Summer 2005 — “I Shall Be Released” clue culture
Listeners interpret music choices, especially “I Shall Be Released,” as coded hints about the expected XM move.

# XM / early satellite — 2005–2007

## September 9, 2005 — BB King's XM kickoff/welcome-back gathering
A Times Square event helps bridge the old WNEW/WJFK community into the new satellite era.

## September 12, 2005 — regular XM broadcasts begin
Ron & Fez join High Voltage 202. Satellite's most important creative advantage is time: interesting conversations can continue without a rigid terrestrial clock.

## November 9, 2005 — Fez suffers a heart attack
The first major satellite-era cardiac crisis begins the documented health history that must remain separate from work/shoot speculation.

## December 2005 — East Side Dave joins the staff
Dave's trajectory will move from producer to essential on-air character, Fez rival, creator, event performer and later independent host/oral historian.

## 2006 — Paltalk Big ASS Room grows in importance
The surviving room listing calls itself **created by the fans for the fans**, says `NO BASHING`, and lists French Bread Pizza as owner.

## November 6, 2006 — probable failed/partial Board Gossip start
A later compilation labels this a failed start. Full-show audio survives but still needs direct segment indexing.

## November 8, 2006 — probable full Board Gossip debut
Same caveat: strong compilation chronology, primary full-show indexing still required.

## December 2006 — Free FM dual-show period begins
Ron & Fez briefly maintain both satellite and New York terrestrial output, revealing how difficult the deep-insider XM continuity could be for casual terrestrial listeners.

# Peak density — 2007–2009

## February 23, 2007 — Big ASS Bash
Hard Rock Cafe Times Square. East Side Dave/Midnight Rider storyline, Tazz, Danny Doring, large civilian/staff crossover, and later Earl ticket controversy. One of the clearest work/real-life hybrid spectacles.

## March 2007 — Gail O dies
Paul O's real personal tragedy enters a culture where some listeners have been trained to doubt apparently factual announcements. The reaction becomes an important example of how deeply work/shoot uncertainty had conditioned the audience.

## May 2007 — Fez has another serious cardiac episode
Another stent is required.

## June 4, 2007 — official metadata directly links Crazy Jen to Paltalk
A small but useful anchor connecting WJFK-era civilian continuity to the mature XM live-room ecosystem.

## June 21, 2007 — East Side Dave and Casey Elan marry at Hard Rock Times Square
A real wedding becomes a major R&F public event. A near-contemporary account says the couple had initially planned something smaller and that Ron encouraged the huge public version.

## 2007 — Night of a Hundred Podcasts
East Side Dave's later oral history remembers Ron using SiriusXM hours to put friends-of-show podcasts onto the actual satellite channel. Exact original date remains unresolved.

## 2007 — RF Softball, bar nights, BBQs, Rock-a-Hula, Winter Carnival
The ordinary physical life beneath the large official spectacles becomes increasingly visible in the restored RFNet gallery.

## December 2007 — “Fez holds court” gallery caption
A Winter Carnival photograph is literally captioned **“Fez holds court with Leticia's crew,”** echoing later listener memory of Fez's Fairfax social role.

## 2008 — civilian/fan world remains deeply integrated
Paltalk, Board Gossip, staff/fan relationships, fan songs, old Florida characters and satellite-era friends form a parallel social system underneath daily radio.

## January 2009 — Black Earl resigns
A nine-year production/cast bridge leaves the show. Dave loses his most natural workplace antagonist.

## 2009 — Dave/Fez rivalry becomes structurally central
Character contests, trivia and Siren Series-style competition raise the question of who is functionally occupying the role beside Ron.

## 2009 — “The Plan” mythology
The notion that Dave will replace Fez becomes believable because of the visible structure. The archive has **not** established a literal secret management plan.

## May 2009 — show expands to four hours
The workload and the Fez participation problem become more visible.

## May 29 and June 1, 2009 — Chuckwagon/Fez priority tapes
Full broadcasts survive and are specifically identified in later archive discussion as central to the Fez/Chuckwagon story. They still need direct segment indexing.

# Ensemble break / social-media migration — 2010

## February 19, 2010 — Tiger Woods Big ASS Simulcast
Direct RFNet archive evidence preserves the live alternative-commentary event.

## March 7, 2010 — second Academy Awards simulcast
RFNet explicitly describes this as the second Oscars simulcast, implying a still-to-be-fully-verified first edition.

## Spring 2010 — Save Dave
Dave seeks improved compensation; fans organize using Twitter-era hashtags including #SaveDave and #DavePound. The community's organizing center is visibly migrating beyond RFNet.

## June 2010 — East Side Dave leaves
Dave exits after the pay dispute. Fez remains. Chris Stanley becomes executive producer.

## August 2010 — RF.net v3 directly evidenced
Original RFNet archive posts casually reference `v3` and embed material from `ronfezv3.com`. Later participants connect v3, Most Post Wins and broader RFNet fragmentation, but no single-cause collapse story is currently justified.

## September 2010 — Fez suffers another heart attack
The post-Dave show enters a smaller and more psychologically focused era.

## December 12, 2010 — Fez fantasy-football / holiday apartment party
Publisher metadata fixes the Sunday date; the December 13 full show survives for aftermath indexing.

---

# The post-Dave show and Fez's coming out — 2011–2012

## December 2011 — Ronnie Spector private SiriusXM Christmas concert
After venue trouble, Ronnie Spector performs inside SiriusXM for Ron, Fez and a small invited group of fans. The old permeability between artist, studio and listener survives.

## February 24, 2012 — Fez comes out publicly
Fez's first-person statement directly connects his identity conflict with depression, anxiety, stage fright, panic and locking up on-air. Ron had known privately beforehand.

## 2012 — coming out does not restore old Fez
The program's central production problem remains. This is evidence against a single-cause theory of Fez's decline.

# Late era / second form — 2013–2014

## May 8, 2013 — Intern Popcorn breakdown
A late-era example of R&F turning the thing a conventional program might remove from the microphone into the conversation itself.

## 2013 — Shelby Royston enters the late cast
Shelby eventually becomes one of Fez's most productive antagonists.

## 2013 — Shelby spends Thanksgiving with Fez's family
Real social proximity; later radio framing exaggerates and sexualizes the relationship. Exact private romantic meaning remains unresolved.

## January 6, 2014 — move to Raw Dog Comedy Hits 99
The practical lineup often becomes Ron, Fez, Chris and Shelby.

## March 2014 — Cakehorn
Original cause remains unresolved. Ron's subsequent escalation is clearly deliberate improvisation.

## 2014 — Steakgate
Current best forensic model: likely constructed premise → genuine Fez/Chris workplace anger → return to performable premise: **WORK → SHOOT → WORK**.

## 2014 — *The Halls*
The show creates an Instagram-native scripted extension around interns, marking another evolution of the secondary-media world.

## July 2014 — Anthony Cumia fired by SiriusXM
The old O&A/R&F shared satellite neighborhood fractures institutionally.

## October 17, 2014 — *Bennington* begins
Ron starts the new program with Gail Bennington while *Ron & Fez* continues. This gives Ron a separate creative future without requiring Fez's forced replacement.

---

# The end — 2015

## April 1, 2015 — Fez retirement announced
The April Fool's Day timing makes many listeners suspect a work. The retirement is real.

## April 3, 2015 — final live *Ron & Fez*
#GoodbyeRonandFez trends; old staff, fans and friends respond. Big ASS numbers remain part of fan identity.

## April 3–10, 2015 — retrospective week
Ron and Fez revisit the program's history.

## April 10, 2015 — final sign-off / *Bennington* future
The program ends rather than replacing Fez with another “Fez.”

---

# Afterlife — 2015–2026

## 2015–2018 — Fez retires to Florida
He remains connected personally and is remembered as a committed *Bennington* listener.

## 2018 — Fez surprise appearance at *Bennington* Thanksgiving event
Hard Rock Times Square provides a physically symbolic return to the old community world.

## August 14, 2021 — Fez dies at age 57
The old community reconvenes across generations.

## August 16, 2021 — major memorial broadcast
Old terminology, handles and emotional rituals return immediately. “Satellite of Love” again carries closing significance.

## 2021–2026 — participant oral history accelerates
East Side Dave's *Tales From the Satellite*, modern Al Dukes appearances, Tasteless Ginny's resurfaced early material, Don the Hypnotist's corrected recollections, B.L.'s continuing Florida history and other participant sources keep changing the record.

## January 2023 — RonFez.net returns as memorial media archive
The site's role changes from active town square to historical museum.

## January 16, 2023 — restored first-RFNet-X-Mas-party image discussion
Old users identify the first party as 2002 and recognize early civilian figures. This becomes an important new lead toward `The Group Photo`, CyberSoldier's holiday pictures and Moshin's early physical-community role.

## January 19, 2023 — FezCon
Hard Rock Times Square hosts the physical memorial/reunion. Old screen names, Big ASS identity and show vocabulary become social passwords again.

## 2023–2026 — synchronized archive listening / historiography
Fans increasingly treat surviving recordings as a historical corpus. Old shows can become temporarily “live” again when listeners press play together.

## 2026 — the forensic reconstruction expands
The surviving RFNet media-address system, old numeric IDs, gallery migration history, Board Gossip tapes, Fez/Chuckwagon tapes, event photo sets, obscure civilian biographies and living participant testimony become active research branches.

The project is no longer trying merely to remember the show.

It is trying to reconstruct **how the whole system worked**.

# Era guide

| Era | Broadcast center | Community form | Main creative mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Florida / Daytona | Ron & Ron → early Ron & Fez | live-event/ensemble roots | people around the show become material |

| 2009–10 | four-hour show | factions + Twitter migration | Dave/Fez rivalry, Plan mythology |
| 2010–12 | Ron/Fez/Chris | distributed fandom | Fez's participation becomes central subject |
| 2013–14 | Raw Dog | interns/social media | psychological micro-comedy |
| 2015 | final R&F | farewell network | relationship itself becomes ending |
| 2015–21 | *Bennington* / archives | diaspora | preservation |
| 2021–23 | memorial era | reunion | collective memory |
| 2023–26 | archive/history era | museum + synchronized listening | reconstruction / oral history |
