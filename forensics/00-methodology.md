# Forensic Methodology

## Purpose

*Ron & Fez* deliberately blurred performance and reality. A normal fan wiki often collapses:

- what actually aired;
- what participants later said happened;
- what fans believed at the time;
- what fans now remember;
- what was intentionally fabricated;
- what began as a work and became genuine conflict.

This project should not do that.

The forensic layer exists to make uncertainty explicit.

## Evidence grades

### A — Direct

Highest-value evidence:

- original broadcast audio/video;
- contemporaneous participant statement;
- official station/network documentation;
- direct documentary record of date/event;
- participant statement made as part of the event itself, with the caveat that an on-air statement may still be a work.

**Use:** establish what was said/done and when.

**Limitation:** even original R&F audio does not automatically prove that the premise being presented was genuine.

### B — Strong corroboration

Examples:

- multiple independent contemporary accounts;
- later firsthand participant testimony consistent with surviving broadcast evidence;
- event photographs plus contemporaneous logs plus participant recollection;
- independent external reporting consistent with show material.

**Use:** very likely reconstruction.

### C — Participant recollection

Later testimony by:

- Ron;
- Fez;
- East Side Dave;
- Earl;
- Chris Stanley;
- Al Dukes;
- Shelby;
- other direct participants.

Participant testimony is extremely valuable but not omniscient. Memory changes, participants know only their own perspective, and R&F veterans may still perform while describing old material.

**Rule:** do not automatically elevate a later Ron explanation to absolute fact if it conflicts with contemporary evidence or is delivered in a context designed to preserve ambiguity.

### D — Contemporary fan record

Examples:

- RonFez.net threads;
- Wackbag threads;
- Paltalk logs/screenshots;
- listening notes;
- contemporary forum posts;
- fan photographs and captions;
- episode logs.

These are excellent for establishing:

- what listeners heard;
- what listeners believed;
- what terminology existed at the time;
- who appeared at an event;
- when a bit entered community vocabulary.

They are weaker for:

- private backstage motives;
- secret management decisions;
- whether a host was genuinely angry unless independently corroborated.

### E — Later fan consensus

Examples:

- Reddit retrospectives;
- YouTube comments;
- oral lore;
- modern archive-listen discussion.

Useful for:

- locating missing material;
- discovering alternate interpretations;
- identifying which moments remained culturally important;
- finding leads for primary-source research.

Not sufficient alone for strong claims about unseen facts.

### U — Unresolved

Use when:

- sources conflict;
- origin cannot be established;
- participants give incompatible accounts;
- available evidence documents the broadcast but not the backstage state;
- “work” and “shoot” cannot be meaningfully separated.

**Unknown is a valid result.**

## Work/shoot vocabulary

### WORK
A premise or event intentionally presented as real despite being constructed or knowingly performed.

### SHOOT
A genuinely occurring event/emotion/conflict rather than a prearranged premise.

### WORK → SHOOT
A constructed premise produces genuine emotion or conflict.

Example candidate: **Steakgate**.

### SHOOT → WORK
A real event is recognized as useful content and deliberately converted into performance.

Example pattern: many Dave/Earl workplace disputes.

### COLLABORATIVE EXAGGERATION
The underlying event is real, but participants knowingly heighten their roles because they understand the radio game.

This is likely common across R&F and should not be forced into a fake/real binary.

### REAL EVENT USED AS BIT
Something genuinely happens without show planning; Ron notices the comic structure and builds a segment around it.

This may be one of Ron's most characteristic methods.

### ORIGIN UNKNOWN / ESCALATION WORKED
Useful when the first trigger cannot be established but the later improvisation is obviously intentional.

Example current classification: **Cakehorn**.

### “RON STILL SCREWING WITH US”
Informal label for cases where later explanations themselves may be performative or deliberately ambiguous. This should never substitute for evidence grading, but it captures a real methodological problem with this particular source base.

## Critical distinction: person versus radio identity

A real person does not imply an unperformed radio persona.

Examples:

- East Side Dave was real; **East Side Dave** was also a heightened performance identity.
- Earl was real; the **Black Earl mythology** was constructed around real traits and events.
- Perrynoid was a real community member who consciously performed a radio identity.
- Fez began as an explicit character and eventually became increasingly inseparable from Todd Hillier's real psychological life.

For each person, ask:

1. Was the human being real?
2. Was the name/persona constructed?
3. Who controlled the persona?
4. Which traits were genuine?
5. Which were knowingly exaggerated?
6. Which relationships were real?
7. Which interpretations were supplied by Ron rather than the person?

## Critical distinction: audience belief is not backstage proof

If contemporary listeners believed something was real, that proves **the work succeeded**, not necessarily that the event was genuine.

Likewise, if listeners thought something was fake, that does not prove it was staged.

Fez's retirement is the clearest example: many listeners and even Earl suspected an April Fool's work; the retirement was real.

## Source-priority rule

When evidence conflicts, prefer roughly:

1. original recording for what actually aired;
2. contemporaneous external documentation for dates/events;
3. contemporaneous private/public participant evidence where available;
4. multiple independent participant recollections;
5. single participant recollection;
6. contemporary fan records;
7. later fan consensus.

But **source type must match the question**.

Example:

- To establish what fans believed on March 4, 2014, a March 4, 2014 forum thread may be better evidence than Ron remembering it in 2025.
- To establish whether Steakgate was prearranged, a later backstage explanation by Ron/Chris may be more useful than fan certainty during the original broadcast.

## Date discipline

Every forensic case should eventually include:

- exact event/broadcast date;
- date of each retrospective explanation;
- how much time separated memory from event;
- whether the source was contemporaneous or retrospective.

This helps prevent later interpretations from being projected backward.

## Civilian privacy and deanonymization rule

The project is intentionally exhaustive, but **“no detail too small” does not mean using modern data-broker tools to expose private identities**.

Many R&F community members participated publicly under handles. Search engines can now surface reverse-email, reverse-phone, people-search, address-history, and other broker pages that were not part of the person's public R&F participation.

### Do not use those sources to deanonymize a fan.

A civilian's real name may be attached to a handle when at least one of the following is true:

- the person publicly self-identifies the connection;
- original RonFez.net/Paltalk/show material publicly identifies them;
- a reputable published source identifies the connection in a historically relevant context;
- the person later participates publicly under both identities;
- multiple strong non-invasive public sources corroborate the identity.

Do **not** publish:

- private home addresses;
- personal phone numbers;
- private email addresses;
- relatives found only through people-search services;
- data-broker identity matches;
- sensitive personal information unrelated to the show/history.

### Handle-only is a valid historical identity

If a person is known to the R&F world only as:

- Msteels;
- Ozzie;
- Hottub;
- Sheepy;
- or another handle,

then the handle remains the archive identity until the person or a reliable historical source establishes more.

This rule protects both accuracy and privacy: data-broker matches are often wrong, and even correct private matches are unnecessary to explain the radio/community history.

### Public notoriety does not erase the rule

When a civilian later becomes a public figure, as with **Lenny McNab / ChuckWagonCook**, the identity may be documented because published reporting and matching public material establish it. That does not justify importing unrelated private data about the person or family.

## Correction policy

Corrections should be explicit.

Example already encountered:

Earlier in the retrospective, **Iris** was treated as though she might have been a naturally eccentric real caller. The forensic pass later identified her as a fictional caller voiced by Ron Bennington.

The correct process is:

1. preserve the original mistaken assumption in provenance if needed;
2. mark the correction clearly;
3. update reference files;
4. do not silently allow both versions to coexist as equal possibilities.

## No-detail-too-small rule

This project's user explicitly wants maximal granularity. Therefore evidence files may preserve:

- exact phrases;
- usernames;
- screen names;
- Big ASS numbers where publicly relevant and appropriate;
- tiny production quirks;
- song choices;
- specific bar names;
- who sat where if documented;
- phone screener details;
- one-off character appearances;
- exact board reactions;
- alternative spellings of nicknames;
- small contradictions across sources.

The purpose is not brevity. The purpose is **recoverability**.

The civilian-privacy rule above is the boundary: exhaustive history should preserve the public R&F world, not reconstruct unrelated private lives from modern surveillance-style data sources.

## Standard case-file template

Each future forensic case should contain:

```markdown
# CASE-XXX — Title

## Question
What exactly are we trying to determine?

## Current verdict
WORK / SHOOT / WORK→SHOOT / SHOOT→WORK / COLLABORATIVE EXAGGERATION / UNKNOWN

## Confidence
High / Medium / Low

## Timeline
Exact dates and sequence.

## Direct evidence
Original audio/video/docs.

## Contemporary fan record
What listeners believed at the time.

## Participant testimony
Later Ron/Fez/Dave/Earl/Chris/etc. accounts.

## Conflicts
Where sources disagree.

## Best reconstruction
Most defensible account.

## What remains unknown
Explicit unresolved questions.

## Sources
URLs / archive identifiers / timestamps.
```

## Central principle

The project should not ask only:

**“Was it real?”**

The better questions are often:

- What part was planned?
- What part was genuine?
- When did the emotional state change?
- Who knew the premise?
- Did the audience know?
- Did Fez know?
- Did staff know?
- Was a genuine event later performed repeatedly?
- Did later retellings rewrite the original event?

That is the level at which *Ron & Fez* becomes historically intelligible.
