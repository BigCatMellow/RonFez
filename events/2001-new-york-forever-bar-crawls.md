# New York Forever — post-9/11 bar crawls and physical community

## Classification

**WNEW-era civic/community response / fan bar-crawl network / lower-Manhattan support campaign / early physical RFNet-era culture**

## Working historical account

Surviving R&F historical summaries say that after the September 11, 2001 attacks, Ron and Fez organized several **bar crawls and other events under the motto “New York Forever”** to encourage listeners to patronize businesses in lower Manhattan.

The **2001 Halloween Bar Crawl** is specifically remembered for a large turnout and chaotic behavior.

Two venues repeatedly named in the surviving history are:

- **The Slaughtered Lamb**;
- **Karavas' Place**.

Sources:
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_and_Fez
- https://www.wikizero.org/wiki/en/Ron_and_Fez

Evidence grade: **B/D historical synthesis**; primary threads/audio still required.

## Independent physical trace: Slaughtered Lamb, March 2002

A surviving RonFez.net gallery image is explicitly titled:

> **slaughtered lamb march 2002**

The uploader, **furie**, registered on RFNet in June 2001 and had more than 17,000 posts in the surviving profile.

Source:
- https://www.ronfez.net/gallery/showphoto.php/photo/2845/ppuser/37110

This does not prove the October 2001 Halloween details, but it directly demonstrates that **The Slaughtered Lamb remained an RFNet/R&F physical gathering location into March 2002**.

Evidence grade: **A/D gallery artifact**.

## Why “New York Forever” matters

The post-9/11 response is one of the clearest places where the R&F community crossed from entertainment into local civic identity.

The basic loop was:

`radio emotional response → listeners gather physically → spend money in affected neighborhoods → new shared stories → radio/community archive`

This is different from a station promotional remote. The stated purpose was tied to supporting lower-Manhattan businesses after a catastrophe.

---

## Connection to September 11 broadcast preservation

RFNet later made an explicit exception to its usual practice and preserved the **entire September 11, 2001 show**, explaining that the broadcast was important enough to hear in full.

That means two community responses to the attacks survive:

1. **archival response** — preserve the entire show;
2. **physical response** — organize New York Forever gatherings/bar crawls.

Together, they show the early community defining itself through both memory and place.

Source for archive policy:
- https://www.ronfez.net/forums/downloads.php?do=cat&id=60

The New York Forever period is probably one of the earliest moments when the R&F audience stopped behaving like an audience and began behaving like **a local social organization with a shared civic identity**.
