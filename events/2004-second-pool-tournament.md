# 2nd Annual Ron & Fez Pool Tournament — 2004

## Classification

**WJFK-era live listener event / billiards tournament / hypnosis showcase / listener-jingle competition / civilian-cast convergence**

## What is currently supported

Multiple surviving historical summaries describe a **2nd Annual Ron and Fez Pool Tournament (2004)** with:

- a billiards tournament;
- **Don the Hypnotist** hypnotizing Fez, Crazy Jen and listeners;
- football trivia;
- a listener jingle contest.

Sources:
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_and_Fez
- https://www.wikizero.org/wiki/en/Ron_and_Fez

Evidence grade: **B/D secondary synthesis**, awaiting primary event thread/audio.

---

## Chronology strengthened by Don's own later records

In September 2026, Don the Hypnotist publicly corrected his own memory after reviewing old files: he had thought his R&F work started later, but found that he was already appearing with the WJFK show in **2004**.

Source:
- https://www.reddit.com/r/ronandfez/comments/1w9dmje/early_pics_ron_fez_show_wjfk/

That makes the 2004 tournament's Don material substantially more plausible than if it rested only on the uncited event list.

However, Don has not yet publicly tied a specific photograph/date to the pool tournament itself.

---

## Event genealogy

The title “2nd Annual” implies a first pool tournament, already separately reconstructed in the event index as a 2003 appearance featuring:

- Fez singing “Harper Valley PTA”;
- Cigar Sid/Sidcada singing “Sugar Sugar”;
- J-Dubs' Hot Pepper Challenge;
- “Fairyoke.”

The second event appears to preserve the same structure:

> **normal bar/game-night premise + deliberately ridiculous show-within-event contests + civilians as performers.**

---

## Why the hypnosis matters

If the historical description is accurate, the tournament may be one of the earliest clearly documented public-event intersections among:

- Fez;
- Don the Hypnotist;
- Crazy Jen;
- ordinary listeners.

This would place the hypnosis relationship earlier than several famous XM clips and shows that a premise later remembered as a satellite-era institution had roots in the Fairfax physical community.
