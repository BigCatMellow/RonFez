# Big ASS Bash — February 23, 2007

## Event classification

**XM-era live event / Hard Rock Cafe Times Square / wrestling-comedy storyline / staff-production crisis / civilian-cast convergence / East Side Dave–Midnight Rider centerpiece**

## Date and venue

The event took place on **Friday, February 23, 2007** at the **Hard Rock Cafe in Times Square, New York City**.

The date is strongly supported by two independent sources:

1. a full R&F broadcast upload titled `2007-02-23 – Day of the Big Ass Bash`;
2. WrestlingInc, publishing Monday Feb. 26, says that “on Friday night” Tazz and Danny Doring participated in the Ron & Fez Big Ass Bash at the Hard Rock Cafe in New York.

Sources:
- https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HuFBEBr3Tw
- https://www.wrestlinginc.com/news/2007/02/dsw-stars-to-raw-496814/

**Evidence grade: B/A-adjacent.**

# Why the Bash matters

Dave McDonald's later oral history calls it a mixture of **WrestleMania and *The Last Waltz***—a useful participant description because the event was simultaneously:

- a live radio/comedy show;
- a wrestling storyline;
- a celebration of the wider R&F cast;
- a fan gathering;
- a culmination of weeks of production/ticket chaos;
- a major chapter in the Midnight Rider/East Side Dave mythology.

Dave's retrospective explicitly lists among the participants:

- Ron;
- Fez;
- East Side Dave;
- Earl;
- Anthony Cumia;
- Dawn Cumia;
- Big A;
- Tazz;
- Danny Doring;
- and others.

Source:
- https://directory.libsyn.com/shows/view/id/a21240b6-43bd-4ac5-a961-10011b8d9f9c

**Evidence grade: C — participant retrospective, reinforced by contemporary wrestling coverage and RFNet photography.**

# The Midnight Rider storyline

WrestlingInc's Feb. 26 contemporary report gives a concise description of the central wrestling angle:

- East Side Dave faced a masked cowboy presented as **the Midnight Rider**;
- Dave himself had been the man behind the Midnight Rider mask in the storyline;
- the live event introduced a **second masked cowboy**;
- the second Rider was revealed as former ECW wrestler **Danny Doring**.

This is direct contemporary outside coverage and therefore one of the best sources for the basic worked premise.

Source:
- https://www.wrestlinginc.com/news/2007/02/dsw-stars-to-raw-496814/

# Tazz and Danny Doring

The same contemporary wrestling report specifically says:

> Tazz and Danny Doring took part in the event.

This matters because their presence was not merely R&F pretending to inhabit wrestling culture. The show imported **actual professional-wrestling personnel** to validate/complicate its own fictional wrestling storyline.

Tazz was also doing work with 92.3 Free FM at the time, tightening the shared radio/wrestling ecosystem further.

# The ticket controversy

Dave's *Tales From the Satellite* later devoted a separate episode—**Episode XLVII: Earl & The Ticket Controversy**—to the Big ASS Bash ticket problem.

The episode description says Earl Douglas “screwed up the tickets” and names a supporting cast including:

- Al Dukes;
- Don Wickland;
- Bobo;
- Mikeyboy;
- Hard Rock Johnny.

This is historically important because the event's **production failure became its own serialized storyline**, separate from the live show itself.

Source:
- https://www.ivoox.com/podcast-davey-mac-s-tales-from-the-satellite_sq_f12733182_2.html

**Evidence grade: C — Dave's retrospective framing.**

# Event genealogy

The Bash belongs to a longer lineage:

```text
Big ASS Night of Fights (2002)
        ↓
Big ASS Night of Fights II (2002)
        ↓
other live/physical WNEW + WJFK event culture
        ↓
XM-era Dave/Earl/Fez wrestling-style storylines
        ↓
Big ASS Bash (2007)
```

The 2007 event is therefore not the invention of live-event R&F. It is the **satellite-era maximal version** of a format the show had been refining since WNEW.

# Work/shoot classification

The central Midnight Rider premise is plainly **worked professional-wrestling-style storytelling**.

But several surrounding elements may have been genuine:

- ticket/logistics failures;
- staff frustration;
- live timing problems;
- audience reactions;
- personal tension among producers.

Current classification:

> **WORKED EVENT FRAME containing potentially genuine production chaos and interpersonal conflict.**

This is exactly the R&F pattern where a planned fictional structure gives real problems somewhere to go.

## Core interpretation

The Big ASS Bash was the XM-era R&F universe treating itself like a **major live franchise**. Real professional wrestlers validated a fake wrestling angle; fans and old characters filled the room; a production/ticket controversy became its own storyline; and the community photographed and debated the event afterward.

It is a strong example of R&F's most characteristic transformation:

> **real logistics + fictional wrestling + genuine relationships + fan participation → one piece of serialized radio history.**
