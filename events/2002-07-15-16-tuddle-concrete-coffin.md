# Tuddle buried alive in concrete — July 15–16, 2002

## Classification

**Cross-show stunt / former-staff continuity / charity event / Central Florida radio spectacle pulled back into R&F**

## Direct surviving R&F archive

RonFez.net preserves a dedicated audio package titled:

> **July 15 & 16, 2002 — Ron & Fez call Tuddle in a Coffin**

Its description says:

- Tuddle was a **former Ron & Fez producer**;
- he agreed to spend **48 hours in a coffin encased in concrete**;
- the stunt was being done for **Monsters in the Midday**;
- the purpose was to **raise money for charity**;
- Ron & Fez called him a number of times while he was inside in order to freak him out.

Source:
- https://www.ronfez.net/forums/downloads.php?do=cat&id=60

Evidence grade: **A/D surviving RFNet archive metadata**.

## Why this matters historically

The stunt is important because it reveals that R&F's cast genealogy remained porous **after people left the show**.

The basic flow is:

`R&F PRODUCER → LEAVES FOR ORLANDO RADIO → NEW SHOW PUTS HIM IN BIZARRE CHARITY STUNT → R&F CALLS HIM INSIDE THE STUNT → NEW-SHOW EVENT BECOMES R&F AUDIO ARCHIVE`

That is more than a nostalgic call to an ex-coworker.

R&F effectively reached into another market and another program's content and **reabsorbed its former producer into the continuing show universe**.

---

## The physical premise

The archived description says:

> 48 hours in a coffin **encased in concrete**.

That wording raises many unanswered safety/logistics questions:

- Was the coffin itself fully surrounded by poured concrete?
- Was there an access tube/air system?
- Could Tuddle exit in an emergency?
- Was the concrete structural/theatrical rather than load-bearing around the coffin?
- Was there medical supervision?
- Where was the stunt staged?
- Could spectators see him via camera?
- How did radio staff communicate with him?

Do not infer these details from the dramatic shorthand “buried alive.”

---

## Charity question

RFNet says the stunt was intended to raise money for charity, but the current source does **not identify the beneficiary**.

That is an important retrieval target because contemporary local press may have covered the stunt under the charity's name rather than Tuddle's.
