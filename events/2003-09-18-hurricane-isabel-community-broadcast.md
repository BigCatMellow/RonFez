# Hurricane Isabel community broadcast — September 18, 2003

## Classification

**WJFK emergency/community-information broadcast / caller network used as local reporting conduit / non-comedy evidence of the show's civic function**

## Why this belongs in the event archive

Most R&F community history revolves around comedy, parties, fights, bars and recurring personalities.

Hurricane Isabel shows the same network functioning in a different mode:

> listeners called the show with real-time information about outages, shortages and conditions while a major storm hit the Washington region.

This is unusually valuable because the strongest source is **not fan memory**. It appears in a U.S. congressional record discussing radio's role during emergencies.

## Date

Hurricane Isabel struck the Washington region on **September 18, 2003**.

The congressional source describes WJFK's programming on “the night Hurricane Isabel pummeled the DC area.”

Working event date:

> **September 18, 2003**

The archive should still verify the exact R&F tape/date against whole-show collections.

## Congressional record description

A U.S. House hearing record describing local radio emergency coverage says that on the night of Hurricane Isabel:

- **The Ron and Fez Show devoted its entire nighttime show to the hurricane**;
- Ron and Fez acted as a **conduit for listeners** to call in and share experiences;
- examples specifically included **power outages and food shortages**;
- instead of returning to normal syndicated programming afterward, WJFK aired a special edition of **El Jefe's Hideout**.

Source:
- https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-108hhrg92537/pdf/CHRG-108hhrg92537.pdf

**Evidence grade: A/B — official governmental record summarizing the station's own emergency-coverage example.**

## Independent R&F historical record

Long-maintained show histories separately remember that Ron and Fez took calls for roughly **two extra hours** during Hurricane Isabel while DC, Northern Virginia and Maryland were dealing with outages/flooding.

Source:
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_and_Fez

Because that detail is fan-derived and is more specific than the congressional record, `two extra hours` remains **B/D pending original tape/schedule verification**.

## Why this is structurally important

The same open-call architecture that usually produced:

- Comedy Pyramid lines;
- recurring callers;
- arguments;
- weird characters;

could be repurposed instantly into:

- outage reports;
- food-access information;
- eyewitness conditions;
- emotional support/community contact.

In other words, the show's audience was already a **distributed local sensor network**.

That phrase should not be over-romanticized: callers can be wrong, anecdotal, delayed or duplicative. But the station explicitly considered the listener input valuable enough to cite it later as an emergency-service example.

## R&F's recent relocation makes the episode more interesting

Ron and Fez had only relocated physically to the WJFK studios in Fairfax in **June 2003**.

By September, only a few months later, they were sufficiently integrated into the DC/Northern Virginia/Maryland audience to spend an entire storm broadcast taking local reports.

That is a much faster form of community integration than the later “outsiders in DC” narrative might imply.

## El Jefe's Hideout continuation

The congressional source says WJFK followed R&F with a special **El Jefe's Hideout** instead of normal syndicated programming.

This is important for two reasons:

1. it places **El Jefe/The Hideout** inside the station's serious local-response infrastructure, not merely as a comedy side project;
2. it shows R&F's relationship with the Hideout in a concrete station-wide context.

The special's exact duration, hosts, callers and content remain open.

## Compare with September 11, 2001

There is a striking but careful comparison available.

### September 11, 2001 — WNEW/New York
R&F stayed on-air taking deeply distressed listener calls after the attacks; RFNet later made an explicit exception to its usual policy in order to preserve the complete show.

### September 18, 2003 — WJFK/DC region
R&F again converts the show into a listener-information/community channel during a major local emergency.

These are different events and should not be equated emotionally. Structurally, however, they show the hosts already had an established instinct:

> **when normal life breaks down, stop doing the normal show and let the audience tell one another what is happening.**

## Core interpretation

Hurricane Isabel reveals the most serious version of the R&F participatory model. The audience wasn't just a source of jokes. In an emergency, **the same open network became a way for local people to report conditions to one another through the radio.**
