# Ronnie Spector private Christmas concert — SiriusXM — December 2011

## Classification

**Private in-studio concert / emergency venue substitution / celebrity-fan intimacy / Christmas tradition preserved through R&F**

## Strong near-contemporary source

The Interrobang published a report on **December 23, 2011** describing an unusual Ronnie Spector Christmas performance.

According to the report:

- Spector had maintained an approximately **two-decade tradition of performing a Christmas show in New York City**;
- a **last-minute problem with one of the clubs** threatened that year's performance;
- an exclusive audience of Ronnie Spector fans instead attended a **private concert in the SiriusXM studios**;
- the event was hosted with **Ron Bennington and Fez Whatley**;
- Spector performed **“Baby I Love You”** and several Christmas songs;
- every audience member received a copy of her Christmas CD;
- one fan with a Ronnie Spector tattoo had Spector sign the tattoo;
- the performance was scheduled to air on *The Ron & Fez Show* that Friday and repeat during a Sunday Christmas special.

Source:
- https://theinterrobang.com/exclusive-ronnie-spector-christmas-concert-for-ron-fez-and-friends/

Evidence grade: **B/A-adjacent near-contemporary reporting from an outlet closely connected to R&F.**

---

## Why this event is unusual

Unlike the Big ASS Bash or bar nights, this event appears to have been created **because another venue failed**.

That means SiriusXM/R&F became emergency cultural infrastructure for an artist whose own holiday tradition was in danger of being broken.

The flow is:

`club problem → Christmas show threatened → R&F/SiriusXM studio becomes substitute venue → small fan audience gets private show → radio broadcast preserves event`

This is very different from booking Spector as a normal interview guest.

---

## Ron's relationship with Ronnie Spector

Ron had a documented admiration for and recurring relationship with Ronnie Spector as an artist/guest. The broader R&F archive repeatedly places her in the show's music/interview orbit.

A 2011 `202friendsinfo` item also notes Spector visiting to discuss her cover of Amy Winehouse's **“Back to Black”** and charity proceeds for Daytop Village Substance Abuse Centers.

Source:
- https://202friendsinfo.wordpress.com/

That helps explain why R&F could plausibly serve as the host environment for a private emergency Christmas performance rather than merely report on its cancellation.

---

## Audience intimacy

The Interrobang describes an **exclusive audience of some of Spector's biggest fans** rather than a large public ticketed event.

The tattoo anecdote is especially revealing. A fan had Spector's likeness tattooed on his forearm and got the artist to sign it during the event.

That creates a particularly intimate music-fandom artifact:

`artist → devoted fan body art → artist signs fan's own tattoo → R&F/Interrobang records encounter`

Research should identify whether that signed tattoo was later permanently inked over/autographed, if the fan publicly documented it; do not attempt to identify the fan privately.

## Music-history significance

This event belongs in the R&F music history because it shows Ron's music curation at its most literal:

- not merely choosing a deep-track rejoiner;
- not merely interviewing a favorite artist;
- but helping turn the **radio studio itself into a temporary concert venue**.

The R&F Christmas identity therefore included live music as well as parody songs, staff performances and fan-created tracks.

## Exact date problem

The Interrobang article was published Friday, **December 23, 2011**, and says the performance could be heard on R&F “this Friday” with a Sunday Christmas replay.

That strongly places the performance during the week of December 19–23, 2011, but the article does not expose the exact recording date in the searchable text.

Therefore the filename remains month-level rather than inventing a day.

Research targets:
- December 19–23 R&F episode metadata;
- SiriusXM schedule;
- photo timestamps;
- original performance audio.

## Core interpretation

The Ronnie Spector Christmas concert captures a side of R&F that gets lost when the retrospective focuses only on meltdowns and civilian drama: **Ron could use the show's relationships and studio access to create a genuinely special music event, preserving another artist's long-running New York tradition when her ordinary venue failed.**
