# Big ASS Night of Fights II — May 17, 2002

## Event classification

**Live WNEW-era Ron & Fez spectacle / fan-community event / staged fight card / civilian-cast convergence / early RFNet physicalization**

## Why this event matters

Big ASS Night of Fights II is one of the best surviving single documents of the early *Ron & Fez* universe because a commercial-free recording survives with a detailed timestamp breakdown.

The event brings together:

- Ron Bennington;
- Fez Whatley;
- Al Dukes;
- Black Earl;
- Billy Staples;
- Hard Rock Johnny;
- French Bread Pizza;
- GVac;
- Matty Fridays;
- Anthony Cumia;
- Eddie Trunk;
- Sweet Melissa;
- multiple Ron & Fez Girls;
- assorted audience volunteers/performers;
- several sideshow-style acts;
- hundreds of fans, including some reportedly left outside because the venue reached capacity.

This means the civilian community was already being incorporated into **formal live-event casting in 2002**, only about a year after RonFez.net's institutional launch.

# Event chronology from the surviving timestamp map

## 00:00 — preshow

The preshow already demonstrates how many layers of the universe are active before the fights begin.

### French Bread Pizza

French Bread Pizza appears discussing a tiny lace bikini.

This is historically important because French Bread Pizza is later directly documented as owner of the **Ron and Fez Big ASS Room on Paltalk**.

Her presence here in 2002 shows that she belongs much earlier in R&F community history than the mature XM/Paltalk era might suggest.

### Al “Glasshead” Dukes

Fans/participants mock Al Dukes, with “Glasshead” used as part of the fight-storyline identity.

The broadcast recaps Al's prior fight, establishing that Night of Fights II is not an isolated stunt but **continuity from the first event**.

### Earl defends Muni

Black Earl defends Muni and discusses the hostility toward Al.

This is a precursor to the main-event Earl/Al conflict.

### Fans pitch Al nicknames

Listeners/community members propose ring names for Al, another example of **audience participation in building the staged wrestling language** around actual staff members.

---

## ~00:31 — upcoming fights and Billy Staples

Ron promotes the upcoming card.

### Hard Rock Johnny

Hard Rock Johnny briefly appears but, according to the later timestamp notes, does a poor job of promoting/hyping his own involvement.

This is an early direct event-placement lead for a civilian/show personality who otherwise remains underdocumented.

### Billy Staples

Billy arrives to promote his fight against Dan.

Ron and Fez build comedy around things Billy has “fought for,” turning the fight setup into an extension of the Billy Staples character rather than a generic boxing match.

The hosts/fans then offer predictions.

---

## ~01:01:55 — formal event begins

The recording's timestamp map describes Ron and Fez entering to a very large audience reaction.

### Billy Staples and the Billy's Angels

Billy arrives with **Billy's Angels**, a presentation layer that deserves separate identification/research.

Questions:
- who were the Angels?
- recurring performers or event-specific?
- relationship to R&F Girls?
- costumes/music?

### Hard Rock Johnny's opponent problem

Hard Rock Johnny was reportedly supposed to face a different opponent, but the planned opponent did not participate. Eddie Trunk was also absent at that moment.

A replacement named **Jasper** was pulled from the crowd.

This is a good example of planned event structure colliding with genuine live-event unpredictability.

### Sweet Melissa

Sweet Melissa audio/moans are used after the fight.

That connects a recurring WNEW sonic character to the physical show.

---

## ~01:30 — R&F Girls / Nature Boy / production problems

### Ron & Fez Girls

The R&F Girls appear, demonstrating the show's recurring promotional/performer layer.

### Nature Boy Fez entrance goes wrong

Fez enters as **The Nature Boy**, but the wrong music is initially played.

The later archival description blames **Rory**, who eventually gets the proper entrance music running.

Fez then performs an unusually long **Jan Brady** promo.

This small sequence is important because it layers:

- Fez playing Nature Boy;
- wrestling entrance music;
- a real production error;
- Fez switching into another fictional character, Jan Brady;
- live crowd response.

The show is already operating on multiple levels of characterhood at once.

### Al and Earl fill time

Al Dukes and Black Earl come out to fill time, again using core staff as live performers.

### Eddie Trunk arrives

Eddie Trunk eventually appears after being absent earlier.

# Billy Staples' Diggstown Challenge

A major event structure is the **Diggstown Challenge**, in which Billy fights a sequence of opponents rather than one conventional bout.

The surviving map identifies several stages:

1. Dan from Hoboken / Heavyweight Craig;
2. Superheavyweight Dennis;
3. New Jersey Rob;
4. Big Show Mike;
5. a final rematch/round with Dan from Hoboken.

The exact rules, round lengths, results and work/shoot nature of each confrontation need direct listening.

The concept itself turns Billy's willingness to endure abuse/physical comedy into a large live-event framework.

---

## ~02:02 — capacity and police/fire-marshal pressure

One of the most revealing details in the surviving description is that **hundreds of fans were reportedly outside** because the venue had reached a point where the fire marshal would not allow more entrants.

The audience was also reportedly warned that overt sexual behavior/flashing could cause authorities to shut the show down.

This needs contemporaneous venue/press confirmation before exact crowd counts are treated as official.

But it strongly suggests the event had become **larger than the room could comfortably contain**.

### Anthony Cumia arrives

Anthony appears to a large reaction and an “Opie sucks” chant emerges.

That demonstrates the close but teasing WNEW relationship between the R&F and O&A audiences.

### Sideshow performances

The event includes sideshow/stunt performers, including sword-swallowing/deep-throating and later body-piercing/glass-breaking acts.

These should be identified by performer and precise act rather than left as anonymous spectacle in the final event history.

---

## ~02:26 — father/son, Billy gauntlet, French Bread Pizza sundae

### Father vs. son

A match is staged between **Andy the Dad** and his son **Brian**, framed around the son being a slacker.

This is another example of importing an actual relationship into a wrestling/comedy frame.

### FoundryMusic figures

**Awesome Garrett** and **Foundry Music Steve** appear.

This is an important reminder that the early online ecosystem was broader than RFNet alone and that FoundryMusic figures crossed into R&F events.

### French Bread Pizza becomes a human sundae

French Bread Pizza is turned into a **human sundae**, with other women/participants eating from the setup.

This is one of the clearest early examples of a civilian fan/community figure becoming a formal event performer.

Given her later Paltalk-room ownership, this event should be treated as a major anchor in her biography.

---

## ~02:55 — RonFez.net Championship: GVac vs. Matty Fridays

This may be the most important single detail for the fan-community history.

The event stages:

> **GVac vs. Matty Fridays for the RonFez.net Championship**

That means by May 2002:

- both handles were recognizable enough to serve as fight-card names;
- RonFez.net itself was recognizable enough to have a mock championship;
- fan status/conflict could be converted into physical wrestling-style spectacle.

This occurs roughly fifteen months after RFNet's working February 2001 launch date.

### Implication for Matty Fridays

This materially strengthens Matty Fridays' early chronology. He was not merely a late-board antagonist remembered in 2010-era schism stories; he was already important enough in **May 2002** to contest the RFNet title at a major R&F event.

A dedicated Matty Fridays file is now required.

### Implication for GVac

GVac's 2016 memorial memories of early bar crawls/softball are consistent with the fact that he was already being used as an identifiable physical-event character in 2002.

## Sideshow act: Jude

The timestamp notes identify **Jude** performing a body-piercing/suspension-style stunt involving hanging objects from his body and putting a nail through his tongue.

This is an obscure but recoverable event performer who should be added to the people/event index.

## ~03:24 — main event: Earl vs. Al

### Special ring announcer: Anthony Cumia

Anthony serves as special ring announcer for the main event.

### Black Earl dedicates fight to Muni

Earl's connection to Muni remains part of the narrative right up to the fight.

### Stalker Patti accompanies Al

**Stalker Patti** comes out with Al Dukes.

This is another early cross-show/civilian-character connection deserving exact context.

### Earl vs. Al Dukes

The main event culminates the Al/Earl storyline.

At one point Earl tears up Al Dukes' master's degree, using a real educational credential as a wrestling prop.

The degree incident should be checked against Al's later retrospective for whether the document was genuine or a prop/copy.

### Final stunt / tape cutoff

A performer named **Pete** breaks glass on his head near the end.

The surviving archival tape reportedly cuts off during the final goodbyes.

That cutoff itself should be documented because it defines what cannot be recovered from this specific source.

# Work/shoot issues

The event is explicitly wrestling-influenced and includes staged personas, arranged bouts and narrative continuity.

That does **not** mean every physical/emotional occurrence was fake.

Questions for later forensic classification:

- Were results predetermined?
- How hard were participants actually fighting?
- What safety rules existed?
- Were replacement opponents genuinely unplanned?
- Was Eddie Trunk's lateness/no-show material worked?
- Was the Nature Boy wrong-music mistake genuine?
- Was the fire-marshal/capacity problem genuine?
- Was Earl tearing up Al's degree prearranged?
- How much of Al's “heel” anger was conscious performance?

The event should ultimately have a moment-by-moment work/shoot ledger.

# Relationship to the first Big ASS Night of Fights

The preshow explicitly recaps **Al's first fight**, confirming continuity with the first event.

Fourble's whole-show archive contains a standalone entry titled **Big Ass Night of Fights** positioned after the Feb. 22, 2002 show and before Feb. 25.

That strongly suggests the first event occurred in late February 2002, but the exact date has not yet been independently confirmed.

## Core interpretation

Big ASS Night of Fights II proves that the R&F secondary universe was already **physically castable** by spring 2002.

The radio staff, fictional personas, web-community celebrities, real fans, neighboring radio personalities and sideshow performers all inhabit one event with no clean boundary between them.

The most revealing match may not even be Earl vs. Al.

It is **GVac vs. Matty Fridays for the RonFez.net Championship**: a radio fan forum had become enough of a social world to stage its own title fight in front of a live R&F audience.
