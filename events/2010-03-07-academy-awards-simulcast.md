# Academy Awards Simulcast — March 7, 2010

## Classification

**Live television simulcast / XM-era annualizing event / staff ensemble commentary / direct RFNet audio archive**

## Direct surviving evidence

RonFez.net preserves two files:

- **2010 Academy Awards Simulcast Part 1**
- **2010 Academy Awards Simulcast Part 2**

Both are dated to **March 7, 2010**.

Part 1's description is especially important:

> **“For the second (and hopefully annual) Oscar awards simulcast...”**

Part 2 explicitly identifies the commentary team as:

- Ron;
- Fez;
- Dave;
- Pepper.

Sources:
- https://www.ronfez.net/forums/downloads.php?do=file&id=3352

Evidence grade: **A/D surviving RFNet archive metadata.**

## What the word “second” proves

The 2010 archive description establishes something stronger than “fans remember another one”:

> **There had already been a prior R&F Oscar simulcast.**

The first edition's exact date/file has not yet been directly located.

A contemporary outside-forum post dated **February 23, 2009** says the writer avoided the Oscars but laughed while R&F mocked everybody on the red carpet beforehand.

Source:
- https://able2know.org/topic/128354-1

Fourble separately indexes a large **February 22, 2009** R&F show file, the date of that year's Academy Awards.

Source:
- https://fourble.co.uk/podcast/ronfezfixedepisodetitles

### Current classification of the first Oscars edition

- A prior formal simulcast existed: **directly established** by 2010 description.
- February 22, 2009 is a **strong candidate** for the first simulcast.
- A contemporary listener confirms R&F red-carpet commentary around that Oscars broadcast.
- We still need the 2009 tape to prove the whole event was formally called the first Academy Awards simulcast.

Do not skip this distinction.

## Why the Academy Awards worked for R&F

The Oscars provided nearly ideal raw material:

- celebrity arrivals;
- visual fashion judgments;
- self-serious Hollywood speeches;
- long dead-air/ceremonial stretches;
- recognizable cultural references;
- unpredictable live mistakes.

R&F could turn an event many listeners were already watching into a **parallel communal comedy program**.

The phrase “hopefully annual” also shows the show itself understood this as a repeatable format rather than an accidental one-off.

By 2010 a simulcast could potentially unfold simultaneously across:

The exact platform mix for March 7 must still be reconstructed, but the format is a mature expression of the show's multiscreen community culture.
