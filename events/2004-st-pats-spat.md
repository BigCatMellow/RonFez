# St. Pat's Spat — 2004

## Classification

**WJFK/Fairfax live listener event / boxing card / physical-comedy contests / civilian-cast convergence**

## Date status

The event is consistently identified in surviving show histories as **The St. Pat's Spat (2004)**. A RonFez.net gallery category also survives under that event name.

The exact calendar date has not yet been independently recovered in this pass, so the file remains year-level rather than inventing a St. Patrick's Day date.

## Current reconstructed card

The surviving historical summary gives these boxing matches:

- **Spoon vs. Tommy Bateman**
- **El Jefe vs. J-Dubbs**
- **Mikey D vs. Wonderboy**

The same summary says **Mikey D quit in the ring** during his match with Wonderboy.

Other contests included:

- **Monster Toss** — a competition to see who could throw intern Monster the farthest;
- **Crazy Jen's attempt to eat 50 hard-boiled eggs**, which she did not complete.

Source:
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_and_Fez

These details currently rest mainly on a long-maintained fan-derived historical summary, so they should be graded **B/D-tertiary pending primary audio/gallery verification**, not treated as equivalent to a surviving ticket or tape.

## Why this event matters

The St. Pat's Spat shows how quickly the Fairfax era developed its own cast.

The card is not built around celebrities. It is built around:

- Spoon
- Tommy Bateman
- El Jefe
- J-Dubbs
- Mikey D
- Wonderboy
- intern Monster
- Crazy Jen

To an outsider, most of those names require explanation. To the active R&F audience, the premise depended on already knowing who these people were.

That makes the event another important stage in the transition from:

> **callers who occasionally appear**

to

> **a recurring civilian ensemble with physical rivalries and event roles.**

## Crazy Jen and the 50 eggs

Crazy Jen's existing biography already contains WJFK-era physical-stunt material, including the July 13, 2004 **Chinese Water Torture** volunteer segment.

The St. Pat's Spat egg attempt supports the larger pattern: Jen's recurring value included a willingness to make **her body the premise of a stunt**.

The challenge also has an obvious *Cool Hand Luke* cultural echo—50 eggs—but the current source does not say whether the show explicitly framed it that way. Do not assume the reference was stated until audio is found.

The St. Pat's Spat is where the Fairfax community starts looking less like a listener base and more like **a local promotion with its own roster**. Ron and Fez could build an entire live event around people whose fame existed largely inside the show's social universe.
