# Early board culture, moderation, and cross-board identity — 2001–2003

## Summary

The idea that RonFez.net only became cliquish, moderated, or part of a multi-board ecosystem during the XM/Board Gossip years is too late.

A surviving **October 2002** discussion on another contemporary board already shows:

- RonFez.net users moving between boards;
- RFNet having a recognizable cultural reputation;
- outsiders describing RFNet as unusually friendly or “candy coated”;
- an RFNet user saying she had nearly been removed multiple times;
- moderation actions such as moving contentious threads into a staff-only area.

This pushes the **cross-board culture war** back several years before East Side Dave formalized it as Board Gossip.

---

## 1. The October 21, 2002 specimen

A surviving thread on `cdih.net` contains a participant using the name **Missy** who introduces herself as coming from what she calls the:

> **“shiney happy ronfez.net board”**

Another poster replies by joking that RFNet people tend to have “candy coated skin” and warns that the new board is rougher.

Missy then discusses expecting a warning email, and the thread contains references to moderation moving another contentious post into a **staff room**.

Source:
- https://www.cdih.net/cdih/showthread.php?pid=153124&tid=3870

**Evidence grade: D — contemporary outside-board discussion.**

The exact social identities behind every poster should not be inferred beyond what they publicly used on the board.

---

## 2. Why this source matters

This thread is valuable because it is **not written twenty years later**.

It captures active users in 2002 already behaving as though each board has a distinct personality.

The contrast being drawn is roughly:

### RonFez.net
- friendlier;
- more moderated;
- less tolerant of personal nastiness;
- recognizable enough to have a stereotype.

### The outside board
- rougher;
- more confrontational;
- proud of being less protected.

That does not prove RFNet was objectively pleasant or the other board objectively hostile. It proves **participants perceived and performed cultural differences between boards in real time**.

---

## 3. Moderation existed early and was socially visible

The discussion references:

- warning emails;
- posts being moved;
- a `staff room`;
- concern about being booted.

This is important for the governance history. By October 2002, RFNet was not simply an unmoderated pile of show comments. It apparently had:

- staff-only moderation spaces;
- disciplinary communication;
- content boundaries recognizable to users.

The exact rules and moderators responsible remain unresolved.

---

## 4. The “nice board” reputation complicates later decline stories

Later users sometimes remember mature RFNet as:

- cliquish;
- negative;
- dominated by insiders;
- increasingly argumentative.

The 2002 source captures a very different stereotype from outsiders: **too nice / too protected**.

This suggests RFNet's culture likely changed over time rather than possessing one fixed personality.

A useful future timeline should ask:

> When does the external stereotype shift from “shiny happy/candy-coated RFNet” to “insider/clique/negative RFNet”?

That transition may help explain later schisms and v3.

---

## 5. Cross-board migration existed before Board Gossip

Missy's introduction assumes that saying “I come from ronfez.net” communicates something meaningful on another forum.

That means users already carried **board citizenship** across sites.

The mature XM-era structure:

`RFNet ↔ Wackbag ↔ FBA ↔ smaller boards`

had an early ancestor by 2002:

`RFNet member → visits another fan/community board → identity follows them`

East Side Dave did not create cross-board culture. **Board Gossip later monetized/dramatized something that had existed for years.**

---

## 6. “Angry Missy” / Missy disambiguation

The thread references both `Missy` and `Angry Missy` in surrounding discussion.

Do **not** assume they are the same person merely because of the shared word `Missy`.

RFNet gallery material later includes **AngryMissy** as a recognizable physical-community figure. This 2002 thread may or may not involve the same identity.

Status: **unresolved alias question.**

---

## 7. Staff-room evidence and archival invisibility

If contentious posts were moved to a private/staff area, some of the most socially important disputes may never have entered public archives or search-engine indexes.

This creates another survivorship bias:

- ordinary public posts survive;
- moderation decisions may be referenced but not visible;
- deleted/moved fights can survive only through quotations on other boards;
- later users may remember disputes whose original evidence is inaccessible.

This is especially relevant to the future Board Gossip reconstruction.

## Core interpretation

By 2002, the R&F internet world already had **borders, citizenship, moderation, stereotypes and migration**.

That means the later Board Gossip era was not the birth of the secondary universe. It was the point at which an already mature social-web phenomenon became an explicit radio format.
