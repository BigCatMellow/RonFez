## Evidence rule

The fan world generated enormous amounts of rumor. For this reason:

- a contemporaneous board post proves **that the claim was being made**, not necessarily that the claim was true;
- later nostalgia proves **what survived in collective memory**, not hidden backstage facts;
- user titles, signatures and jokes are preserved as cultural artifacts but are not automatically literal biography;
- exact identities and private relationships are included only where publicly established by participants or reliable records;
- calling someone a “stalker,” “crazy,” “liar,” etc. is recorded as an attributed show/community characterization unless independently established;
- modern people-search/data-broker sites are not used to deanonymize old handles;
- a restored RFNet gallery date is **not automatically an event date**.

## Current strongest findings

1. RonFez.net's surviving 2011 tenth-anniversary announcement dates the site's institutional beginning to approximately **February 5, 2001**.
2. The site's surviving mature statistics show **38,387 members, 84,226 threads and 3,043,602 posts** accumulated over its life.
3. Founding-era soundboards survive from **June, August and November 2001**, showing fan remix culture almost immediately.
4. The archive explicitly broke its usual no-full-show practice to preserve the complete **September 11, 2001 broadcast**.
5. Paltalk still lists the **Ron and Fez Big ASS Room**, says it was **created by fans for fans**, and names **French Bread Pizza** as owner.
6. A surviving 2006 Board Gossip compilation identifies **Nov. 6, 2006** as a failed start and **Nov. 8** as the full debut; Fourble separately confirms complete daily audio survives for those dates.
7. An independent March 9, 2007 forum discussion already quotes **“Razzle Dazzle … BOARD GOSSIP”**, establishing the segment as recognizable fan vocabulary within roughly four months of the proposed debut.
8. Official XM episode metadata confirms Board Gossip as an established recurring segment by May–June 2007.
9. Wackbag's archived structure contains dedicated R&F discussion and daily listening-thread sections.
10. Contemporary O&A recaps confirm **Full Blown Aids / fullblownaids.com** as an active board/media community integrated into the XM feedback ecosystem.
11. A **contemporary October 2002 outside-board discussion** already characterizes RFNet as a distinct, more protected/“candy-coated” culture and discusses warnings/staff-room moderation. Cross-board identity predates Board Gossip by years.
12. GVac's memorial provides participant memories of **2001 bar crawls**, early rf.net friendships and years of offline social continuity.
13. RonFez.net's 2011 Irish Alkey funeral appeal demonstrates the board functioning as a real-world mutual-aid network.
14. The 2011 tenth-anniversary event explicitly celebrated **friendships found, members lost and good times had**—a useful self-description of what the board had become.
15. A surviving August 2010 RFNet page directly embeds an asset from **ronfezv3.com**, proving the offshoot existed during the original board's active life.
16. B.L. / Brenda Lee is now documented back to the **Ron & Ron / Hooters on the Radio** era rather than being treated only as a later R&F caller.
17. Official June 4, 2007 XM metadata directly confirms **Crazy Jen was on Paltalk**.
18. ChuckWagonCook is strongly identifiable as later *Food Network Star* winner **Lenny McNab**, allowing his old R&F/RFNet history to be cross-checked against outside 2014 media coverage.
19. Surviving RFNet gallery pages show high-volume civilian identities such as **Hottub (41,468 posts), Snoogans (36,871), spoon (35,762), Mikeyboy (33,389), Don Stugots (28,742), and Tenbatsuzen (27,906)**, illustrating how much accumulated history a single handle could carry.
20. WJFK listeners independently remember finding volunteer streams and recordings through RFNet, making the fan system an **unofficial distribution layer** as well as a social network.
21. A photographed original ticket establishes the first **Big ASS Night of Fights** as Feb. 22, 2002 at **Downtown Bar & Club, 190 Main Street, Farmingdale**, with 5 PM doors, 21+ admission and a warning that a ticket did not guarantee entry.
22. An official U.S. congressional record describes R&F devoting the **Hurricane Isabel** night to local listener reports about power outages and food shortages, followed by special local coverage from *El Jefe's Hideout*.
23. The current *Hideout Radio Archives* documents a daughter community called **The Heretics**, who built their own fan boards, fought MySpace battles and created audio/video. The WJFK/R&F participatory model did not stop with R&F.
24. J-Dubs is strongly identifiable as **Justin Johnson**, later a multi-market programmer and 98 Rock Baltimore Program Director, via the Hideout archive's `@dubsradio` link and independent professional radio profiles.
25. **Monty Love** is independently documented as a real Staten Island pop-punk/hardcore band with WFMU airplay in 2003 and New York punk-scene history, strengthening the significance of its R&F live-event appearance.
