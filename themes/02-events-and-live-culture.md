# Thematic File — Events, fights, simulcasts, meetups, and the physical R&F world

## Why events matter

The live-event history is not a side appendix. *Ron & Fez* repeatedly used physical gatherings to collapse the distinction between broadcaster, staff, fan, caller, friend, and character.

Events performed several functions at once:

- they turned screen names into faces;
- they created off-air incidents that could become future radio;
- they gave fans status and proximity to the show;
- they let fictional/worked storylines become real physical spectacles;
- they strengthened listener-to-listener relationships;
- they generated photographs, recordings, songs, and board lore;
- they produced new characters simply by placing strange people together.

This file currently preserves all event material already developed in the originating conversation. A later research pass should expand it into an **event-by-event calendar with date, venue, participants, surviving audio/video/photo evidence, and work/shoot notes**.

## Pre-R&F roots: *Ron & Ron* live-event culture

Ron Bennington's event instinct predates *Ron & Fez*. The *Ron & Ron Show* held large public appearances and touring comedy events during its Florida years. Ron Diaz later recalled some gatherings attracting roughly **15,000 people**.

This is important because the later Big ASS events did not emerge from nowhere. Ron already understood that radio could produce a **physical social community**.

## WNEW / New York era

### New York Forever / post-9/11 gatherings

After September 11, the show participated in gatherings and bar-crawl culture associated with **New York Forever**, encouraging listeners to return to lower Manhattan businesses.

A remembered 2001 Halloween bar crawl included the Slaughtered Lamb among the venues associated with this period.

These events had unusually strong community meaning because listeners had just experienced September 11 through the same radio and board network.

The show therefore moved from entertainment community to shared civic/emotional experience and then back into physical social gatherings.

## Big ASS identity and event culture

The **Big ASS Card / All Secret Society** gave the fan community a formal-but-ridiculous membership identity that translated naturally into live events.

The card established:

- membership numbers;
- recognizable status;
- on-air ritual;
- occasional sponsor benefits;
- a physical artifact fans could bring into real-world gatherings.

Years later, people still referenced Big ASS Card numbers at final-show and memorial events, demonstrating how durable the identity became.

## WJFK/Fairfax physical culture

The Fairfax years may be the most socially intimate period in the show's history.

The smaller local environment made repeated face-to-face interaction easier. A listener could plausibly move from caller to event regular to studio visitor to friend of the show.

### 2003 New York Reunion

A post-WNEW reunion brought former personalities and listeners together and helped maintain continuity after the show's geographic move.

### St. Pat's Spat

The **2004 St. Pat's Spat** is remembered as a deliberately chaotic participatory event involving staff/listener boxing, odd competitions, and physical comedy.

Material associated with the event includes:

- boxing/fight components;
- “Monster Toss” / throwing-an-intern-type spectacle;
- Crazy Jen attempting to eat 50 hard-boiled eggs;
- other fan/staff performance elements.

This is the R&F event formula in miniature: a radio premise becomes a physical event, which becomes radio history.

### Pool tournaments

Pool-tournament events mixed ordinary competition with show personalities, listeners, hypnosis, jingle/music elements, and social interaction.

The contest itself was less important than the density of people from different layers of the R&F universe occupying the same place.

### Battle of the Local Unsigned Bands

The show also used live music/band events, connecting the fan community with the program's broader interest in music discovery and local performance.

This subject belongs in both the event file and the future music file.

### Fez charity races

Fez participated in charity races/5K-type events with listeners during the Fairfax era. These represent another form of physical community that was neither formal comedy show nor generic station appearance.

## Friday Night Lights

**Friday Night Lights** is one of the most important recurring social institutions in R&F history.

By 2005, Fez regularly met listeners and friends on Friday evenings at a Fairfax bar called **Thursdays**.

The gatherings mattered because they were not simply scheduled promotional appearances. Fez was spending personal/social time with listeners.

### Content cycle

`Friday gathering → something happens → multiple witnesses → Ron often not present → Monday investigation → board debate → calls → more radio`

This gave Ron an ideal role as investigator because he could reconstruct off-air events from conflicting accounts.

### Final Friday Night Lights

The final Friday Night Lights occurred on **July 29, 2005**, the same date as the final WJFK broadcast. Ron reportedly attended this final gathering.

That date therefore ends two Fairfax systems at once:

- the local terrestrial show;
- the recurring Fez/listener social ritual.

## XM era: the event machine gets larger

Satellite gave the show national reach while New York once again provided a dense physical entertainment environment.

The existing fan community, staff, O&A universe, wrestlers, comedians, and venue relationships could now converge in larger spectacles.

## Big ASS Bash — 2007

The **Big ASS Bash** at the Hard Rock Cafe in Times Square is one of the clearest diagrams of the full R&F culture.

The major storyline involved **East Side Dave versus the Midnight Rider**.

The Midnight Rider was part of a wrestling-style worked storyline, but the event itself involved actual venue logistics, performers, professional wrestlers, staff responsibilities, and live audience participation.

People associated with the event included figures from multiple branches of the universe, including:

- Ron;
- Fez;
- East Side Dave;
- Black Earl;
- Anthony Cumia;
- Dawn Cumia;
- Big A;
- Tazz;
- Danny Doring;
- fan/community participants.

### Why it is forensically useful

The event demonstrates the R&F work/shoot continuum:

`planned wrestling fiction → real event → actual production stress → performed conflict → possible genuine conflict → future radio`

A clean real/fake binary cannot capture it.

## Dave and Casey's wedding — June 21, 2007

East Side Dave married Casey Elan at the **Hard Rock Cafe in Times Square** on June 21, 2007.

The wedding became:

- a real marriage ceremony;
- a radio event;
- fan gathering;
- photo archive;
- continuing storyline.

RonFez.net preserved extensive photographs of the ceremony, setup, Ron and Fez, and members of the surrounding community.

This is one of the strongest examples of private life becoming public R&F continuity.

## Fan parties and ordinary gatherings

Not every important event had a formal title.

The secondary universe also depended on:

- bar nights;
- birthday gatherings;
- bachelor parties;
- holiday parties;
- staff/fan social events;
- Paltalk-related gatherings;
- informal meetups after broadcasts.

These may be harder to reconstruct because they live largely in photo captions, board threads, participant memory, and later oral histories.

A complete event history should **not privilege official events over informal ones**, because the informal gatherings often generated the next day's radio.

## Simulcasts

The show also created **Big ASS Simulcast**-style experiences around outside media events.

The originating conversation identified preserved examples including:

### Tiger Woods apology

R&F provided live commentary around Tiger Woods' public apology, effectively becoming an alternate audio layer over a widely watched cultural event.

### Academy Awards / Oscars

The RonFez.net archive preserved a **2010 Academy Awards simulcast**, described at the time as their second Oscar simulcast.

### Why simulcasts matter

Simulcasts merge several R&F traditions:

- synchronous communal listening;
- audience participation;
- pop-culture commentary;
- a shared external event providing structure;
- the show's ability to create a parallel experience around mainstream media.

This is conceptually similar to modern live reaction streams and watch-alongs.

## Ronnie Spector studio Christmas event — 2011

When a planned New York Christmas performance ran into venue trouble, **Ronnie Spector** performed a private concert inside the SiriusXM studios for Ron, Fez, and a small invited fan group.

This late event demonstrates that the old physical-community model still existed even after the peak RonFez.net/Paltalk era.

A major artist, the hosts, and ordinary listeners could still occupy one unusually intimate radio-social space.

## *Bennington* Thanksgiving surprise — 2018

After retirement, Fez unexpectedly appeared at a *Bennington* Thanksgiving show at the Hard Rock Cafe in Times Square.

The location carried years of R&F history.

This appearance is important because Fez did not return to restart his career. He returned to **the people and ritual of the community**.

## FezCon — January 19, 2023

**FezCon** at the Hard Rock Cafe in Times Square was a major posthumous community reunion and memorial.

The event drew longtime listeners, old personalities, friends of the show, and people traveling from across the United States.

The attendee culture revived old identity markers:

- screen names;
- Big ASS numbers;
- Whatley Posse references;
- Cakehorn;
- blueberries;
- stand-alone fruit;
- Cookie;
- Nature Boy;
- old caller/character language.

FezCon is therefore both memorial and historical reenactment of the old R&F social structure: people who knew each other as voices/usernames finally or once again occupied the same room.

## Event taxonomy for future research

Every event should eventually be tagged by type:

### `OFFICIAL-LIVE`
Station/show-promoted public event.

### `COMMUNITY-SOCIAL`
Listener/friend gathering with meaningful show crossover.

### `WORKED-SPECTACLE`
Physical extension of an on-air storyline.

### `BROADCAST-SIMULCAST`
Live commentary around an external event.

### `PRIVATE-BECOMES-PUBLIC`
Real personal milestone incorporated into radio, e.g. Dave's wedding.

### `MEMORIAL/LEGACY`
Post-show community reunion or remembrance.
