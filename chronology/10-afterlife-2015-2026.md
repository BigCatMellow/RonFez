# Part 10 — 2015–2026: retirement, Fez's death, FezCon, and the audience inheriting the show

## Summary

The strangest thing about *Ron & Fez* after 2015 is that it did not really disappear. Fez stopped broadcasting but remained attached to Ron, Gail, Chris, and the new *Bennington* show. The audience kept listening to old recordings. When Fez died at 57 in August 2021, people from different generations of the program reconvened as though an old neighborhood had called everyone home.

The fan community then changed function: from **production system** to **preservation system** to **memorial community**.

## Retirement appears to have worked

Fez retired to Florida and largely stayed out of daily radio.

Later accounts indicate he remained a dedicated *Bennington* listener and was supportive of Gail Bennington taking the co-host role.

This reversal is important. For years Fez had appeared threatened by the idea of someone occupying his chair. Once he was no longer responsible for being Ron's co-host, that threat seems to have lost much of its force.

He could simply be Ron's friend and a listener.

## *Bennington* as retrospective evidence

Ron with Gail demonstrates something about the late R&F problem: Ron did not require constant conflict around co-host participation in order to do compelling conversational radio.

Without Fez's performance difficulties, the program could move conventionally among:

- comedy;
- movies;
- music;
- culture;
- guests;
- personal stories;
- Chris Stanley's contributions.

This supports the interpretation that late *Ron & Fez*'s strange meta-format was an **adaptation to Fez**, not merely Ron's default preferred radio format.

## Fez remains in canon

Retirement did not erase Fez from the broader social world. He remained connected personally and culturally.

In **2018**, Fez surprised fans by appearing at a *Bennington* Thanksgiving show at the Hard Rock Cafe in Times Square.

The location was especially resonant because the Hard Rock had hosted major R&F history, including Dave's wedding and Big ASS events.

Fez returned not because he was rebuilding a radio career but because the community remained part of his life.

## August 14, 2021: Fez dies

Fez Whatley died at age **57** in August 2021 after a lifetime of serious cardiac problems. Ron announced his death and described Fez's heart as having finally given out.

The response immediately reactivated the entire historical R&F network.

## The memorial response

Public tributes came from:

- former staff;
- comedians;
- radio colleagues;
- old characters;
- listeners from WNEW;
- listeners from WJFK;
- XM/SiriusXM listeners;
- fans who discovered the show only through archives.

Some people still identified themselves by old **Big ASS Card numbers**.

Others described marriages, friendships, and family relationships that had originated through the show and its fan culture.

This is evidence that the community was not only parasocial.

It created real listener-to-listener relationships that outlived the program.

## August 16, 2021 memorial broadcast

*Bennington* devoted essentially a full show to Fez.

Listeners who had not tuned in live for years returned. Online discussion revived old R&F language immediately:

- “budday”;
- “budday girl”;
- “cousin budday”;
- Cakehorn references;
- Steak 'n Shake;
- old bits and character references.

When “Satellite of Love” closed the show, many listeners described a strong emotional reaction.

The community temporarily reconstructed something close to an old live listening thread.

## Parasocial and genuinely social at once

A useful distinction:

### Parasocial

`listener → broadcaster`

The listener feels a personal connection to someone they mostly know through media.

### Social

`listener ↔ listener`

R&F generated actual friendships, dating, marriages, meetups, and long-term relationships among audience members.

The show's fan culture combined both forms unusually strongly.

## The work/shoot mystery survives Fez

Even during memorial discussion, Ron did not simply provide a total backstage answer key.

When old incidents such as Cakehorn resurfaced, ambiguity remained part of the culture.

This suggests that “what was real?” was not merely a promotional gimmick for active radio. The uncertainty had become part of the show's artistic legacy.

## A better taxonomy than real/fake

The post-show retrospective supports a richer classification:

- **Pure work** — knowingly invented premise.
- **Real event used as a bit** — something genuine happens and Ron recognizes the comic structure.
- **WORK → SHOOT** — a fake premise produces genuine emotion.
- **SHOOT → WORK** — real conflict is deliberately converted into entertainment.
- **Collaborative exaggeration** — participants know the underlying truth and heighten their roles.
- **Unknown** — evidence does not allow a conclusion.
- **Ron still screwing with us** — explanations themselves may remain performative.

## Recovery of early Fez

After Fez's death, memorials and archive listening brought renewed attention to the earlier performer:

- Fezetorials;
- characters;
- impressions;
- rapid one-liners;
- old WNEW and WJFK material.

This helped correct the historical distortion created by late “Silent Fez” dominating memory.

A complete history must preserve **both Fezes**: the highly active early performer and the later psychologically complicated co-host.

## Dave's oral history

East Side Dave's ***Tales From the Satellite*** became an important post-show oral-history source, using old R&F audio and Dave's recollections to revisit:

- Dave versus Earl;
- Big ASS Bash;
- Dave's departure;
- Fez;
- Ron;
- old Christmas material;
- staff and friends of the show;
- numerous specific satellite-era incidents.

Methodological caution: Dave's recollections are **participant testimony**, not omniscient history. They should be compared against Earl, Ron, Chris, surviving broadcasts, and contemporary fan records.

## FezCon — January 19, 2023

A memorial event called **FezCon** was held at the Hard Rock Cafe in Times Square.

The attendee list included recognizable names and identities from the old universe, including figures such as Davey Mac, BL, Iris references, Happy Typing Girl / Debbie Wolf, Anthony Cumia, and many fans identifying themselves through old screen names or Big ASS Card numbers.

People traveled from across the United States, including some who explicitly said they were attending alone to finally meet listeners they had known online.

This physically recreated the old promise of RonFez.net:

**you know these people without necessarily having met them; go to the event and turn usernames into faces.**

## R&F folklore at FezCon

Attendee comments and event culture still referenced old passwords and jokes:

- Cakehorn;
- blueberries;
- stand-alone fruit;
- Big ASS cards;
- Whatley Posse;
- Cookie;
- Nature Boy;
- old caller cadences.

A joke no longer needed the original episode to function.

It had become **folklore**.

## RonFez.net returns as memorial archive

In January 2023, the old site returned as **The Fez Marie Whatley Memorial RonFez.Net Media Archives**.

The restored material focused on the media archive rather than fully reviving the old interactive forums.

That is symbolically important:

Earlier RonFez.net was **where the community lived**.

The restored version became **a museum showing where the community lived**.

## Archive as personal memory

Old event photographs and usernames carry more than show history. For longtime users they preserve:

- old friendships;
- former relationships;
- jobs and apartments;
- people who have since died;
- places that no longer exist;
- a specific era of internet culture.

The archive therefore documents both R&F and the youth/adulthood of the people who used it.

## Synchronized archive listening

By 2026, fans were still organizing synchronized listens to old episodes and discussing them together.

The broadcast is no longer live, but the community can **make it live again** by pressing play together and recreating the old commentary layer.

This is a remarkable final stage of the show's lifecycle.

## What the show ultimately was

A conventional description—“a comedy radio show hosted by Ron Bennington and Fez Whatley”—is technically correct but insufficient.

A fuller description:

> *Ron & Fez* was a long-running improvisational social world in which hosts, employees, callers, friends, and an unusually involved internet community continuously converted one another's real lives into serialized entertainment.

Ron supplied the ability to see narrative structure.

Fez supplied contradictions and a relationship deep enough to remain inexhaustible.

The audience supplied memory.

## Why Fez was not merely the weak link

The late stereotype can flatten the show into:

- Ron = genius;
- Fez = damaged co-host Ron carried.

The chronology does not support that as a complete account.

Fez's unusual personality was precisely what allowed Ron's investigative style to operate at such depth.

Fez could be:

- fast;
- brutal;
- sentimental;
- furious;
- gullible;
- terrified;
- performative;
- sincere;
- evasive;
- embarrassed;
- loving;
- self-destructive.

Sometimes within one broadcast.

A smoother co-host might have created a healthier workplace. It probably would not have created *Ron & Fez*.

## Ron performing for Fez

A memorial-era listener recollection says Ron sometimes described doing portions of the program almost for one audience member: **Fez**.

This fits a recurring audible pattern. When Fez broke into uncontrollable laughter, Ron often continued pushing the line, adding double entendres or absurd associations specifically to break him again.

Even with thousands of listeners, sometimes the most important audience member was sitting three feet away.
