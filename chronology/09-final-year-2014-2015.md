# Part 9 — 2014–2015: the last year, Bennington, retirement, and the end of the show

## Summary

By the final year, it was clear that *Ron & Fez* could not continue indefinitely in its existing form. But Ron did not simply replace Fez because the partnership was not an ordinary employment arrangement. Fez described Ron as his best friend, and at retirement called him friend, partner, brother, and “magic genie.”

At the same time, Ron had already begun another SiriusXM program, *Bennington*, which proved he could pursue a different radio format without first forcing Fez out.

The final irony was perfect: after years of training listeners to distrust the boundary between work and shoot, Fez announced his retirement on **April Fool's Day** and many listeners did not believe it.

## Two Ron Bennington shows by late 2014

In October 2014, Ron began ***Bennington*** with his daughter Gail Bennington. The first broadcast was **October 17, 2014**.

This is important because it demonstrates that Ron had another creative outlet before *Ron & Fez* ended.

*Bennington* could operate as a more conventional conversational program without repeatedly turning the co-host's inability to participate into the central topic.

This should not automatically be interpreted as a secret succession plan. What it does show is that Ron no longer faced a binary choice between:

- continuing *Ron & Fez* forever;
- removing Fez so Ron could keep doing radio.

He could do both.

## Late Fez still generated unique radio

The final era cannot be reduced to “Fez could no longer perform.” He continued to generate material nobody else could have produced.

### The matchbook

A major late-era story involved a sentimental Christmas present: a matchbook Fez said he had preserved from the night he first met Ron.

The emotional meaning was enormous—physical evidence of the beginning of a decades-long friendship.

When doubts emerged about whether the matchbook actually came from that night, Ron treated the tiny object as a serious historical mystery. Fez's explanations generated additional inconsistencies and the investigation became the comedy.

This is classic late R&F:

**the object is not inherently funny; the explanation under scrutiny is funny.**

### Skippy the cat

Fez claimed his cat Skippy could respond to commands, make particular noises, and give “kisses.” When the cat was brought into the studio, it largely failed to cooperate.

Ron did not abandon the segment. The failure itself became the subject.

The mechanism:

`Fez makes claim → reality does not support claim → Ron asks reasonable question → Fez explains → explanation creates more questions → Fez becomes increasingly frustrated`

This is the same structure Ron had used for years with Black Earl and later with Fez himself.

## Failure as content

Late R&F repeatedly proved that failure could be more productive than success.

If Skippy performed perfectly, the segment would have ended quickly.

Because Skippy did not, Fez's insistence that the cat normally did became the real material.

Likewise:

- silence could become content;
- failed character attempts could become content;
- bad explanations could become content;
- a dubious sentimental object could become content.

The show no longer required successful performance in the conventional sense.

## Why Ron kept Fez

Several explanations are supported to different degrees.

### Strongly supported

- Ron and Fez were genuinely close friends.
- Fez explicitly described Ron as his best friend and later as friend/brother/partner.
- Fez remained capable of generating extraordinary radio despite inconsistency.
- Ron found ways to work around Fez's limitations.
- Fez ultimately presented retirement as his own decision.

### Not established

- a secret Ron/SiriusXM plan to replace Fez;
- that Ron was simply waiting for Fez to quit;
- that Fez refused to leave despite Ron demanding it.

The better frame is not “why didn't Ron fire an underperforming employee?” but:

**when do you tell one of your oldest friends that the thing defining his adult life is over?**

## Fez as family

Post-show retrospectives describe Fez as deeply embedded in the Bennington family's life, functioning as an uncle-like figure to Ron's children and remaining part of the family socially long after the show ended.

This makes “replace Fez” the wrong conceptual model.

You could replace a producer.

You could replace Dave.

But removing Fez would not repair *Ron & Fez*. It would create a different program.

That is ultimately what happened.

## March 2015 feels like a farewell in hindsight

Listeners who revisited the final weeks later noticed a large number of recurring comedians and friends appearing during March 2015. Because Ron later said he and Fez had been discussing the retirement decision for some time, those final weeks can sound retrospectively like an extended goodbye, even if not every booking was deliberately planned that way.

The audience experienced a sudden announcement; Ron and Fez apparently did not experience a sudden decision.

## April 1, 2015

On **April Fool's Day**, Ron announced that *The Ron & Fez Show* would end that Friday and Fez would retire from radio.

Fez became emotional explaining the decision and emphasizing the importance of the show and his relationship with Ron.

The audience reaction was predictably suspicious.

After years of:

- false premises;
- worked exits;
- ambiguous staff fights;
- Cakehorn uncertainty;
- Steakgate;
- Earl lore;
- Dave lore;

listeners had been trained to ask:

**Is this real?**

Even former producer Earl Douglas later wrote that he initially assumed the announcement was an April Fool's prank.

## The show becomes a victim of its own epistemology

The most consequential factual statement the show could make—**the show is ending**—was delivered on the calendar's most famous lying day.

This created the perfect final expression of the work/shoot culture.

Audience disbelief did not mean the event was fake. It meant the audience had been successfully trained to distrust appearances.

## April 3, 2015: final live show

The final live *Ron & Fez* aired on **April 3, 2015**.

The hashtag **#GoodbyeRonandFez** trended nationally, and former staff, comedians, friends, and listeners posted farewell messages.

Many messages were less about favorite bits than about the show's role in everyday life:

- commutes;
- jobs;
- marriages and divorces;
- adolescence and adulthood;
- friendships created through the fan community;
- family members who listened together.

This reinforced the central community finding: *Ron & Fez* was both parasocial and genuinely social.

## Big ASS identity at the end

Some farewell messages still referenced old **Big ASS Card numbers**, more than a decade after the membership concept had emerged.

The physical/ritual artifact had survived as a marker of identity long after its practical function mattered.

## “Satellite of Love”

Lou Reed's **“Satellite of Love”** carried special emotional weight as part of the show's closing tradition and final-farewell memory.

The song connected the satellite-radio era, the show's sonic identity, and the final goodbye.

Music was never merely filler on R&F; this will receive a dedicated thematic file.

## Retrospective week

After the final live show, SiriusXM aired a week-long retrospective hosted by Ron and Fez, revisiting major eras, shows, characters, and incidents.

This was effectively the program's own condensed historical retrospective.

On **April 10**, Ron gave the final sign-off and announced the future of *Bennington*.

## *Bennington* takes over

*Bennington* became the full-time successor in the old weekday space, with Ron, Gail, and Chris Stanley.

The continuity is revealing:

- Fez leaves;
- Ron continues;
- Gail becomes co-host;
- Chris remains.

The show changes identity rather than pretending Fez can simply be swapped for another equivalent person.

## Core conclusion

The end of *Ron & Fez* supports a simple but important interpretation:

**Fez was not replaceable within the premise of *Ron & Fez*.**

The unusual relationship between Ron and Fez was itself the show's central structure. Once Fez could no longer sustain the job, the correct outcome was not “new Fez.” It was a new program.
