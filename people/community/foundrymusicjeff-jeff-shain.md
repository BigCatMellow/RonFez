# FoundryMusicJeff — Jeff Shain

## Classification

**Principal early RonFez.net operator / technical-board authority / founding-era community figure / later Weird Medicine contributor**

## Identity

A 2016 memorial entry for GVac is visibly signed:

> **FoundryMusicJeff Shain**

The writer says he met GVac through *Ron & Fez* and that both were part of the **early days of RonFez.Net**, which he calls the first message board for the show.

Source:
- https://www.doctorsteve.com/gvac-memorial/

This establishes a strong public identity link:

> **FoundryMusicJeff = Jeff Shain**

Evidence grade: **C/B participant self-identification.**

---

## Contemporary proof of RFNet authority — October 11, 2002

On another talk-radio board in October 2002, FoundryMusicJeff posted during an O&A-board crisis and explicitly wrote about:

- **“how I run things at Ronfez.Net”**;
- **“my board”**;
- moderation rules at RFNet;
- **“my board software”** being used at OA.com;
- wanting that software removed from the other site.

Source:
- https://www.cdih.net/cdih/showthread.php?pid=363247&tid=3733

Evidence grade: **D/A-adjacent contemporary first-person statement.**

This is currently the strongest direct source for Jeff's role.

### Safe conclusion

By October 2002, Jeff was clearly a **principal operator** of RonFez.net and had technical authority connected to the message-board software.

### Still not proven

The source does not by itself prove that Jeff was:

- the sole founder;
- sole domain owner;
- sole administrator from launch onward;
- the only software developer involved.

Those distinctions remain open.

---

## Moderation philosophy

Jeff's 2002 post is unusually valuable because it preserves **why** he says RFNet was moderated differently from more hostile adjacent boards.

He argues that rules are necessary because, without limits, communities become personally abusive and stop being fun.

A separate October 2002 outside-board discussion independently characterizes RFNet as comparatively “shiney happy” / “candy coated” and references:

- warning emails;
- moderator action;
- threads being moved to a staff room.

Source:
- https://www.cdih.net/cdih/showthread.php?pid=153124&tid=3870

So the board's gentler reputation was not purely later nostalgia. It was already an explicit point of cross-board cultural conflict in 2002.

## Technical role

Jeff's phrase **“my board software”** raises a major historical possibility: he may have written or controlled software used by both RFNet and OA.com.

Current evidence supports **software authority/ownership claim** but not yet the exact architecture.

Questions:

- custom code or modified commercial package?
- same codebase on RFNet and OA.com?
- server/hosting responsibilities?
- database migration history?
- relationship to FoundryMusic.com?

This should be investigated as part of the early-internet technical history, not only fan sociology.
