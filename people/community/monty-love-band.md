# Monty Love — band / Jay Miller

## Classification

**Staten Island pop-punk/hardcore band / WJFK-era R&F live-event performer / New York local-scene bridge / later fan-memory deep cut**

## Why Monty Love matters

Monty Love is easy to misremember as a fictional R&F phrase or one-off local novelty act. Independent music sources establish that **Monty Love was a real New York-area punk band with its own local following and releases**.

That changes how their R&F event appearances should be understood: Ron & Fez were not merely putting a fan with a guitar onstage; they were intersecting with an actual local punk scene.

---

## Independent music identity

Last.fm's preserved event metadata describes **Monty Love** as a **pop-punk/hardcore band from Staten Island, New York** and gives the band-member stage names:

- **Monty** — vocals
- **Gravy** — bass
- **Debo** — drums
- additional guitar role listed in related concert-history sources as **Money**

Source:
- https://www.last.fm/event/82520%2BMonty%2BLove%2Bat%2BDock%2BStreet%2Bon%2B22%2BDecember%2B2006/lineup

Concert Archives similarly describes the band as Staten Island pop-punk/hardcore and says it drew local audiences at venues including **Dock St. on Staten Island** and **The Temple in Brooklyn**.

Source:
- https://www.concertarchives.org/bands/monty-love

**Evidence grade: B — independent music archives.**

---

## Frontman Jay Miller / Brooklyn Punk Temple

A 2008 retrospective about the Brooklyn punk venue **The Temple** identifies **Jay Miller, also known as Monty Love**, as someone who:

- handled the stage during shows;
- set up microphones/checks;
- worked in promotion;
- dealt with newer local bands;
- led his own band Monty Love.

Source:
- https://ffanzeen.blogspot.com/2008/09/memories-of-punk-temple.html

This is a useful near-contemporary scene-history source and places Monty Love within the labor/social infrastructure of the local punk community, not merely as performers.

## WFMU airplay — May 29, 2003

WFMU DJ Pat Duncan's playlist for **May 29, 2003** includes:

> Monty Love — **“Money Shute/Livin Diane”**

from a **Monty Love / Five To Go split CD**, dated 2003, with `montylove.com` listed.

Source:
- https://wfmu.org/playlists/shows/7635

This gives the band independent college/freeform-radio documentation in exactly the period when R&F were building the Fairfax event scene.

## Formal release history

Concert-history sources describe at least one formal release on **Immigrant Sun Records**, titled:

> **Girls Are the New Boys**

The band also released self-produced material. Apple Music currently carries a later 2022 release titled **You Want What We Got (21st Birthday Version)**, suggesting archival/reunion activity decades later.

Sources:
- https://www.concertarchives.org/bands/monty-love
- https://music.apple.com/us/album/you-want-what-we-got-21st-birthday-version-single/1622489518

The exact original discography should be reconstructed from archived montylove.com pages, Discogs/music databases and surviving CDs rather than inferred from current streaming availability.

## Dave & Buster's memory

A July 2026 fan asked the R&F community to identify a punk-ish band they remembered seeing at a **Ron & Fez live show at a Dave & Buster's in 2003 or 2004**. The listener said they liked the group enough to buy multiple CDs at the show, later lost those CDs, and still remembered fragments of songs decades afterward.

Other fans identified the band as **Monty Love**, and the original poster immediately confirmed that was the band they had been trying to remember.

Source:
- https://www.reddit.com/r/ronandfez/comments/1v598uo/i_have_a_crazy_long_shot_question_for_you_guys/

**Evidence grade: C/E — firsthand attendee memory plus community identification; exact event/date pending.**

This is particularly valuable because it tells us the R&F event functioned as **actual music discovery** for at least one listener, not merely background entertainment.

## Why the connection matters to the music chapter

The user specifically remembered R&F taking pride in finding good music and deep tracks.

Monty Love provides a local-band version of that behavior:

1. local band appears in the R&F event world;
2. listeners hear them in a context they would not necessarily encounter otherwise;
3. at least one listener buys their CDs;
4. decades later the sound remains memorable enough to prompt a community search;
5. independent music archives confirm the band had a real scene history.

This is **music curation as community infrastructure**, not just rejoiner selection.

## Core interpretation

Monty Love is an excellent reminder that R&F's secondary universe included **real local art scenes**, not only odd callers. A listener could go to an R&F event for Ron and Fez, discover a Staten Island punk band, buy the records, and still be trying to identify those songs twenty-three years later.
