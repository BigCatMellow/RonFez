# Crazed

## Classification

**Shared O&A/R&F civilian personality / liner voice / rapper/movie-review recurring contributor / late-2000s friend-of-show figure**

## Why Crazed matters

Crazed is a good example of the shared-satellite ecosystem producing a civilian personality who could be rejected by one show and immediately converted into material by another.

The surviving XM metadata catches that process almost perfectly.

## March 31, 2009 broadcast — kicked out of O&A, then used by R&F

A surviving publisher page is labeled **April 1, 2009**, but its own program summary explicitly gives the broadcast date as **March 31, 2009**. That discrepancy should be preserved rather than silently normalized.

The summary says:

- Crazed comes in to talk about **getting kicked out of Opie and Anthony**;
- Crazed then **cuts liners for the Ron & Fez Show and other shows on Sirius XM**.

**Evidence grade: A/B — publisher/XM metadata.**

### Tiny but important metadata anomaly

The webpage title/release presentation says April 1, while the summary says `[Broadcast Date: March 31, 2009]`.

Possible reasons:

- publication one day after broadcast;
- archive labeling issue;
- time-zone/feed conversion;
- duplicated release date.

The archive should retain both fields.

## What a “liner” means

A **liner** is a short prerecorded station/show identification, promo, or transition phrase. Having Crazed record liners means his voice was being deliberately incorporated into the station's sonic vocabulary, not merely tolerated as a caller.

This is an unusual promotion path:

`O&A rejection → R&F conversation → voice used in station/show imaging`

## Later oral-history continuity

Dave McDonald's *Tales From the Satellite* later used Crazed as a special guest in an R&F Christmas retrospective, showing continued connection to the old satellite cast/community.

This is participant-retrospective evidence rather than original R&F documentation.

## Fan-lore leads

Later listeners remember several recurring Crazed motifs, including phrases/labels such as:

- “on the ones and twos”;
- “Crazed-dozer”;
- odd food/vegan material;
- lottery confusion;
- an origin story involving encountering Ron near/in a cigar shop and needing to use a telephone.

These are **E-level retrieval leads** only. They should not be summarized as facts until original audio is recovered.

## Why Ron's use of Crazed is interesting

R&F could turn social awkwardness or rejection into a **different status** rather than simply repeating O&A's framing.

If someone gets thrown out of another studio, Ron can ask:

> Why did that happen?

Then, instead of ending the interaction, the show can give the person a bizarre new function—such as cutting liners or reviewing movies.

That is classic R&F recontextualization.

## Core interpretation

Crazed is a compact illustration of the XM “neighborhood”: **a real civilian personality could circulate between shows, message-board social life, physical gatherings and station imaging until his identity belonged to the entire ecosystem rather than one program.**
